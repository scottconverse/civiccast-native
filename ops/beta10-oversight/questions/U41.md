## 2. Why the worker cannot see "a reload is being prepared" (OBSERVED)

Preparation happens **entirely inside the daemon, before any command reaches the worker**. The
worker's only knowledge of a replacement is the `reload <path>` control line that arrives *after*
`SourcePreparer` has finished.

* The worker's control vocabulary is closed — `civiccast/egress/gst/worker.py` handles exactly
  `swap`, `reload`, `caption`, `stop`; the engine dispatches the same four. There is no "I am
  preparing" verb to send or to receive.
* The supervisor→worker channel carries no marker for it: `strategy.py:_default_worker_launcher`
  (~678-681) only *strips* `SWAPS`/`INTERVAL` from the environment, argv is
  `[python, worker.py, <graph.json>, <control channel>]` (strategy.py:980), and
  `EncoderStartRequest` (`civiccast/egress/encoder_strategy.py:25-77`) has no live/persistent flag.
* The field timeline is the proof: in `evidence\education-exit0-2354\`, the replacement's preparation
  was queued at **23:53:33** and the worker reached EOS at **23:54:41** with
  `pending_reload=False` — **no `reload` command existed at any point in that window**. The engine
  was not "reaching the end of its plan while a reload is being prepared"; it was reaching the end of
  its plan with *nothing in flight anywhere*, because the preparation was still running somewhere the
  worker cannot see.

So the only honest predicate available at the EOS is `self._pending_reload is None` — "no reload
transaction is in flight". That is precisely what the committed hold tests, and it is why the hold
fires in the incident's shape (correct: the channel would otherwise go dark for ~3 minutes) **and**
in the pin's shape below (where the pin says the opposite).

## 5. The decision I need (options; my recommendation is (A))

**What should a worker do at a plan EOS with nothing in flight, when the channel it belongs to is
ACTIVE?** "ACTIVE" is a daemon-side notion (`state=ON_AIR`), which is why the answer probably has to
be carried to the worker rather than inferred by it.

* **(A) Arm the hold from the supervisor — a new "this channel is live/persistent" signal.**
  A fifth control verb (or an argv/env flag on the worker launch) sent by the daemon when it starts a
  live channel; the hold fires only when armed. The beta.5 pin is untouched and keeps its meaning;
  the field gets the protection. Cost: protocol blast radius (`worker.py`, `engine.py`, `strategy.py`,
  `daemon.py`), plus its own tests, and a compatibility story for a worker launched by an older
  daemon. **Recommended** — it is the only option that expresses the requirement as written ("while
  the channel is ACTIVE") without weakening a baseline pin, and it puts the fact where the fact lives.
* **(A′)** Gate the hold on "this worker has already received a `reload`" (`self._rollover_seen` or
  similar). No protocol change, no pin edit — but a live channel whose **first** plan is shorter than
  the lead is unprotected, i.e. the incident's exact shape recurs on a cold start. Cheap, incomplete.
* **(Y)** Ship the unconditional hold as committed and make the beta.5 pin's termination explicit
  (`native._send(control, "stop")` before the `wait`). Cheapest and it deletes no coverage — but it
  edits a *baseline* pin that asserts a product behavior on purpose, so it is your call, not mine.

My recommendation is **(A)**, with (Y) as the acceptable one-line answer if you'd rather keep the
protocol closed and treat the pin's implicit termination as an artifact of the harness.

One fact that makes (A) smaller than it looks: **the product already has the discriminator, it is just
implicit and pointed the wrong way.** `worker.py:557` reads `SWAPS`; a non-zero value runs
`engine.run(swaps, interval_s)` — the finite smoke mode whose documented contract is "swap ``swaps``
times then stop", which does **not** arm the hold (`engine.py:3368` vs the arming at `engine.py:3444`
inside `run_forever`) and therefore keeps exit-at-plan-EOS. Production never sets `SWAPS`, so the
*default* is the persistent mode and the pin inherits it by saying nothing. Making the persistent
intent explicit (a `PERSISTENT`/`CHANNEL` env or control line, or simply arming the hold on
`control_fifo is not None`) inverts that to: explicit-persistent → hold; anything else → today's
behavior, and the pin keeps both its playlist and its exit.

## 7. Secondary (not blocking): Work 1's diagnosis reads differently from the brief's

Work 1's order — "make the automation issue the next rollover as soon as the previous one settles
whenever the remaining plan is shorter than the lead" — was implementable and **is implemented and
green**. But the measurement of the incident does not support the brief's implied diagnosis ("the
rollover fires too late"), so you should know what the fix did and did not buy:

* OBSERVED: the next rollover was issued at **23:53:28** against a plan that ended at ~**23:54:48**
  ("the live plan **ends in 80s**"), and the preceding rollover was "**accepted … awaiting
  settlement**" at **23:42:07**. So the plan the engine took air with had a total life of ~**83 s**
  (the previous deferred switch ran the full 11 minutes to its boundary at ~23:53:25).
* INFERRED (from the code and the absence of any dispatch or warning in those 11 minutes): the
  decisive gate was the silent issued-latch + pending-settlement return at
  `civiccast/egress/automation.py:1916-1922` — while any reload is settling, no new rollover is even
  considered. The horizon trigger (`automation.py:2007`, clamped at 2003-2007) was already due.
* Therefore the dispatch happened **2 s after the plan took air** — the earliest the ~2 s automation
  poll allows. The runway was not lost to a late dispatch; **the plan was simply shorter than the
  lead**, and no lead rule can give an 83 s plan a 690 s lead. (The D43 cadence floor did not delay
  this dispatch either: the floor here is `min(300, 0.5 × 83) ≈ 41.5 s` measured from a dispatch 692 s
  earlier, long expired.)
* What the committed change therefore does: it removes the *last* remaining gate between "the previous
  rollover settled" and "the next one is dispatched" (the D43 floor, which can hold a fast cycle back
  for up to half the plan's life — the case `tests/egress/test_automation.py:471` pins), and it makes
  the log say **why** the lead was short instead of reading like a mistimed dispatch. It does not, and
  cannot, restore the 80 s.

If the coordinator wants the *runway* itself fixed, that is a third question (should the daemon accept
