## 15. Q12. The loop's trim step assumes a limiter slope of 1.00; it measures 0.538–0.615 on the cells that fail

**OBSERVED.** All seven cells report `converged = True`, and the two SUSTAIN cuts land **0.6–0.7 LU
below −16.0** on the whole program. The cause is arithmetic, not the curve:

* `trim0 = TARGET − I_A`, where `I_A` is measured on a pass with **no limiter and no trim**;
* the correction is `trim1 = trim0 + (TARGET − I_B)` — a unit-slope Newton step;
* the measured limiter compression slope `(I_C − I_B)/(trim1 − trim0)` is **0.538–0.615** on 4D36 and
  BIG, and 0.667–1.000 on the five cells that pass;
* `residual = (I_B − TARGET) × (1 − slope)` predicts the measured miss to within 0.1 LU on both
  failing cells (predicted 0.600 both; measured −0.700 and −0.600).

Two aggravating facts, both OBSERVED: `I_A` **rises 0.5–0.7 dB per iteration** on those cells as the
curve is corrected, so `trim0` walks from +0.0 to −1.9 dB without ever accumulating a correction;
and `I_C` moves by at most **0.1 LU** across every iteration of every cell, so your stop rule ("no
window moves > 0.1 LU") is satisfied at a **pinned fixed point** that is not the target. The loop
cannot see the difference between converged and pinned.

**Q12.** Is fixing this in scope for U25, or is it a new unit? The three candidates I can see:
**(a)** accumulate the trim across iterations instead of recomputing it from an unlimited pass;
**(b)** estimate the limiter slope each iteration and use `trim1 = trim0 + (TARGET − I_B)/slope`,
with a floor on `slope` to avoid a divide-by-near-zero;
**(c)** replace the whole trim step with a bounded solve (bisect the trim over 2–3 passes) and keep
the stop rule as an emitted-loudness test rather than a window-movement test.

I recommend **(b) plus a floor and a change of stop rule to "emitted whole-program error ≤ 0.1 LU or
no improvement"** — it is the smallest change that addresses the measured cause, and (a) alone does
not, because the slope error is present in the first iteration too. But note this is a change to the
loop's mathematics after four rounds, so it is your call whether it stays in U25. **Nothing
implemented.**

## 24. Q15 — the design question this round actually produced

The loop converges its gain curve by measuring **at a stage it does not ship**. It measures at the
nominal −1.50 dBTP ceiling; the guard then emits at a lower ceiling; nothing re-converges. The
measured consequence is §23.4: the stage alone passes all 7 loudness gates but only 2/7 TP gates; the
guard alone reaches 5/7 TP but breaks the loudness on 3 cells.

Options, with what I know about each:

- **(a) Two-phase, converged at the shipped ceiling.** Choose the ceiling first (a cheap one-pass
  probe), then re-converge the trim at that ceiling. Correct by construction, costs ≈2× the loop.
  Timing headroom exists (1.72× / 2.16×), so this is affordable. **I recommend this as the
  correctness backstop.**
- **(b) Bound the guard's ceiling drop.** Cap how far the guard may lower the ceiling and document
  the residual TP breach. Cheapest, but it ships the artefact that fails the gate it exists to pass.
- **(c) Ship a lower a-priori ceiling on every asset.** Every asset pays; the loudness cost is the
  same −0.1 to −1.2 LU the guard already measured, now paid unconditionally.
- **(d) Pick the ceiling up front from the measured pre-AAC TP plus a per-asset encoder overshoot**
  (decision 3's "cache it", generalised: the loop already measures pre-AAC TP, and §23.1 gives the
  encoder's contribution on the offending frame). The guard stays as a rare outer safety net rather
  than the primary control. **Cheapest per asset, and it uses a number the loop already computes.**

**My recommendation: (d) as the primary, (a) as the backstop for the cells (d) misses.** But this is
a product decision — how much loudness drift is acceptable to buy a certified TP — and I am not
picking it silently.

Two smaller questions this round also raises, answerable independently:

- **Q15a.** The guard's control loop is **not monotone** (§23.3): a 1.2 dB lower ceiling produced a
  +0.30 dB *higher* emitted true peak on 4D36. Is a single-shot re-emit still the intended shape, or

- **Q15b.** §23.6 shows the oversampled limiter holds **inter-sample** peaks that the old stage
