## The four options, with their costs

1. **Chain two plans at rollover.** When a rollover's target is filler, dispatch
   the preparation of the program *after* the filler at the same moment, and
   build the rollover plan so it carries filler + next program. Keeps the daemon's
   "one plan per channel" invariant — the plan's *contents* grow, the slot count
   does not. Touches `automation.py` rollover construction and `source_plan.py`
   plan assembly. **Lowest blast radius of the four.**
2. **Hold N prepared plans (enable a look-ahead queue).** Turn on a look-ahead
   provider and make `_active_prepared_plan_dir` a bounded queue. This is the
   path `automation.py:2768` says was disabled to protect the 24 h-proven
   automation path; it also reaches into the engine's switch/rollover path.
   **Highest blast radius.**
3. **Make the lead adaptive.** `lead = worst-case prep of the asset about to air`,
   computed from its duration and a measured realtime factor, instead of
   `2*timeout + 30 + 60`. This is a *policy* change and needs the owner's number
   for the factor to assume (item 2 measured 18.6–23.8× on this box under load;
   the live module docstring, `preparer.py:93-107`, records 16.3× uncontended /
   866 s under contention). It fixes the "in time but late" band; **it does not
   fix 18:04**, where the plan on air was already 155 s long when the decision
   was made.
4. **Accept a predictable slate.** When nothing can be ready at the due moment,
   air the slate at due time and keep preparing. Honest, tiny code change, but it
   changes on-air product behaviour — the opposite of "no program may ever start
   late".

**Recommendation: 1 + 3.** Option 1 is the only one of the four that satisfies
"hold the NEXT program prepared" without changing the invariant the 24 h-proven
path rests on; option 3 is the number that decides *when* to dispatch and should
be set from measured conform cost rather than from the timeout. Option 2 is the
"proper" fix and is the one I would do if the owner is willing to re-open the
look-ahead decision — but that is an owner call, not mine.
