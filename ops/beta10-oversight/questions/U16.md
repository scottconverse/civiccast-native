## 2. The engine mechanism, stated exactly (with the reason it is undecidable from here)

`_begin_reload_commit` (`civiccast/egress/gst/engine.py:3112-3164`):

- one value for every new-leg pad: `switch_running_time = max(observed)`, where `observed` is the list
  of per-pad `state["end"]` from the outgoing probes (`_on_outgoing_pad_data`, `engine.py:2664`), each
  computed in **that pad's own cached segment** by `_buffer_end_running_time` (`engine.py:2643`);
- fallback when `observed` is empty: `self._pipeline_running_time_ms() * Gst.MSECOND`;
- `pad.set_offset(switch_running_time)` for each pad of the new leg, wrapped in
  `contextlib.suppress(Exception)`;
- then the selector handoff and the hold release; `_confirm_reload_selector_handoff` (`engine.py:3001`)
  must then observe the new leg's flow or the commit wedges.

A single shared value applied to both pads is **symmetric**: it cannot by itself produce a one-sided
A/V displacement. The only *asymmetric* inputs to it are the two pads' own observed ends. So a
one-sided +108.95 s requires that, at 20:15:02, the audio pad's own observed end differed from the
video's by ≈109 s, and that the audio was placed at the video's value (or vice versa) — i.e. exactly
the brief's premise, with the per-stream divergence as the trigger rather than the shared value as the
cause.

The problem is the alternative policy. I can argue both ways, and the evidence I have cannot separate
them:

- **A delivery lag** (the lagging stream's last *delivered* buffer is behind because its own queued
  backlog has not drained). Here the shared `max` is *correct* — the lagging stream's continuation is
  its own end **plus its backlog**, so resuming it at its own last end would start it behind its own
  queued data. Measured (x9): a natural ~0.75 s per-pad spread produced **no** output step at all
  (`|a-v| ≤ 0.033 s`), i.e. the shared max behaved exactly right.
- **A genuine timeline divergence** (one branch withheld/stalled — U15's C3, which I did reproduce:
  x8 K=30 = the video pad records no end and the audio's value is borrowed ⇒ the worker logs
  `CTRL reload: commit did not finish within 15s - quitting for daemon restart` with the *same thread
  signature* as the two live wedges; x10 lead=20 s = the offset 9.984 s applied when the pipeline's own
  running time was 1.914 s ⇒ `CTRL stall: no output for 10s - quitting for daemon restart`, the live
  stall signature 73× in the live stderr log). Here a per-stream own end (or a clamp) is what avoids
  the one-sided shift. But in both of my constructions the *proximate* cause of the wedge is visible in
  the construction itself (a jammed paced sink; a finite successor consumed into a 20 s queue), so
  neither run proves the offset was what broke the live channel.

From the per-pad ends alone, a delivery lag and a genuine divergence **look identical** — that is the
blocker. Choosing `max` or own-end or a clamp is a policy decision with a measurable cost either way
(the clamp's cost is bounded by the pipeline's queued lead, ~0.7 s in the healthy paced case), and it
would be shipped on a guess.
