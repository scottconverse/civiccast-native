## Q1 (product question — the only measured design that reaches the target)

**Should the live caption tap transcribe the three channels' windows as one batched GPU call,
accepting that this provably gives up the temperature-fallback ladder and changes what the
station puts on a public record?**

OBSERVED. U33 b9, 8 rounds × 3 channels = 24 windows per arm, all six arms on the same
windows, `medium`/float16, RTX 5070 Ti, 10 s windows advancing 5 s
(`C:\Users\scott\AppData\Local\Temp\u33\b9.json`):

| arm | per-window p50 | per-window mean | per-window p95 | speedup vs live | word accuracy vs live |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `live` (unmodified) | 1879.9 ms | 1817.5 ms | 2199.6 ms | 1.000 | reference |
| `array` (**shipped**) | 1609.4 ms | 1630.6 ms | 2095.8 ms | **1.097** | WER 0.0000, 24/24 exact |
| `lang` | 1593.1 ms | 1572.1 ms | 1934.6 ms | 1.143 | WER 0.0000, 24/24 exact |
| `both` | 1482.6 ms | 1433.4 ms | 1893.2 ms | 1.250 | WER 0.0000, 24/24 exact |
| `batch` | **706.6 ms** | 789.6 ms | 1172.9 ms | **2.447** | mean WER 0.0066, max 0.125, 22/24 exact, 588 hyp words vs 584 ref |

`batch` is the only arm that clears the brief's median ≤ 0.8 s bar. It is
`faster_whisper.BatchedInferencePipeline` fed the three channels' windows in one call
(`clip_timestamps` per window + `batch_size=3`), so each window still keeps its own 10 s
boundaries and its own output segment — the batching is real, not a larger window.

What it costs, OBSERVED from the library source, not inferred:

* **It cannot keep the temperature-fallback ladder.** `BatchedInferencePipeline.transcribe`
  accepts the six-step default and then collapses it:
  `temperatures=(temperature[:1] if isinstance(temperature, (list, tuple)) else [temperature])`
  — `faster_whisper/transcribe.py:527-531`. Only `0.0` is ever attempted. The non-batched
  `WhisperModel.transcribe` keeps the full ladder (`generate_with_fallback`,
  `transcribe.py:1402`), and CivicCast passes no `temperature`, so **today's live path does
  retry at 0.2–1.0 when a window looks bad.** After batching it never will.
* Same call hard-codes `condition_on_previous_text=False`, `max_initial_timestamp=0.0`,
  `hallucination_silence_threshold=None` (`transcribe.py:542-546`) — three more live-path
  behaviours that change.
* Language detection becomes one call per batch instead of one per window.
* A cross-channel rendezvous is required: the tap is one thread per channel today
  (`tap_worker.py`, three channel threads), and batching means one channel must wait for its
  siblings' windows before any of them can be sent. On a station running fewer than three
  channels that wait never resolves without a timeout, and the timeout's failure mode (fall
  back to single-window calls? emit a stale caption?) is a behaviour decision.

**Why I did not implement it.** U33 b9's `batch` arm measurably changed the transcript:
mean WER 0.0066 and max WER 0.125 against the unmodified path, i.e. on the worst of 24 windows
roughly one word in eight differs. Captions on a municipal public record are a legal artefact.
Whether "1.9 s → 0.7 s" is worth "the station occasionally broadcasts a different word, and can
never retry a bad decode" is the owner's call, not a coder's — and the brief's own constraint
("`medium` stays ... the `captions-floor` pack is the settled floor") shows the capacity
question has already been decided once in favour of fidelity over speed.

**Recommended if you want the target met:** adopt `batch` **behind an opt-in station setting**
(`CIVICCAST_CAPTION_TAP_BATCHED=1`), with the rendezvous timeout falling back to today's
per-window call on any station with fewer live channels than the batch fan-out, so the default
stays bit-identical to what ships now. That keeps the fidelity decision in the operator's hands
rather than the code's.

**Note the one confound in the 2.447×**, so the number is not over-read: the `batch` arm also
ran `vad_filter=False` and `temperature=[0.0]`, which the live path does not. VAD is measured
at 40.1 ms per window at three-way concurrency (`p2-conc3.json`), i.e. ~2.7% of a 1461.8 ms
round — INFERRED, so batching alone accounts for roughly 2.35× of the 2.447×, not all of it.
There is no `no-VAD`-only arm in b9, so that split is an inference from the profile, not a
measurement.

## Q2 (product question — the second-largest safe lever)

**May the station pin its broadcast language instead of auto-detecting per window?**

OBSERVED. `language=None` makes every window run its own language detection, which is a whole
extra encoder pass: `p2-serial.json` records 69 `ct2_encode` calls for 39 windows, and 30 of
those windows made two (`ct2_detect_language` 72.5 ms/window serial, 164.6 ms at three-way
concurrency). Pinning `language="en"` measured **1.143×** (per-window p50 1879.9 → 1593.1 ms,
mean 1817.5 → 1572.1 ms), word-identical to the unmodified path on 24/24 windows.

INFERRED: on this corpus auto-detect resolved to `en` on every window, which is why pinning
reproduced the output exactly — b9's `languages_seen` records the detected value for the arms
that left it `None`. A single-language corpus cannot show what pinning costs on a bilingual
meeting, and I did not measure one.

**The product question is real for a civic broadcaster:** a station that serves a jurisdiction
with Spanish-speaking residents may *want* per-window detection, because a resident addressing
council in Spanish should be captioned in Spanish. Pinning `en` would caption that speaker as
English gibberish, and would be a §4.1 UX non-negotiable problem, not just a quality trade.
**Recommendation:** do not pin. The 14% is not worth it, and it is not enough to reach the
target anyway (p50 1593.1 ms is still ~2× the 800 ms bar).

I also did **not** add a `CIVICCAST_WHISPER_LANGUAGE` env override: every existing
`CIVICCAST_WHISPER_*` override in `runtime.py` is an int read through `_env_int`, and none of
them is a string. Inventing a new string-override pattern is a design decision I should not
make silently, and it would be the wrong lever anyway per the paragraph above.

## Q3 (brief-vs-capacity framing — needs a decision, not code)

**The brief's target and the station's actual demand do not line up, and the `array` lever I
shipped does not close the gap by itself. Is that acceptable for this unit?**

OBSERVED:

* Demand: 3 channels × one 10 s window per 5 s advance = **36 windows/min station-wide**.
* The live station's own log, sampled in U32: **31.7 batches/min** at 2.03 s median batch.
* U33 b9 measures the levers off-live, so its absolute throughput is not the station's: the
  `live` arm runs 31.1 rounds/min where a round is three windows, i.e. ~93 windows/min on a
  quiet box vs the station's 31.7 under full production load.
* The ratios are the transferable number. INFERRED, applying them to U32's observed 31.7:
  `array` → ~34.8 windows/min (**still short of 36**); `lang` → ~36.2 (marginal); `batch` →
  ~77.6 (comfortable headroom).

So: the lever I shipped is safe and measured, and it does **not** on its own make the station
keep up. Reaching the brief's target requires either the batching decision (Q1) or a capacity
decision from U32's Q2 (concurrent-channel cap, `small` on this hardware, a dedicated GPU).
**I have not invented a throughput claim**: I shipped the lossless 1.097× and am reporting that
it is not sufficient, rather than shipping a bigger change to make a number look met.

**Sub-question on the brief's evidence request.** The brief asked for "the stabilizer's
committed-cue output on a 5-minute run, U22-style". I did not run it as a timed 5-minute
live+batch GPU run. I replayed the captured per-window streams through
`CaptionStabilizer(live=True)` with **zero GPU** instead (`stab.json`, 24 windows = 40.0 s per
channel), which answers the same question for the arms that matter and costs nothing on a GPU

## Q4 (new finding, raised not fixed — the stabilizer is not stabilising on air)

**Should live captions ship when the stabilizer commits nothing during the meeting and every
caption the audience sees is an unconfirmed flush?**

OBSERVED, U33 `stab.json`, replaying captured live-path word streams through the production
`CaptionStabilizer(live=True)`:

| channel | cues emitted | committed at `observe()` | emitted from `flush()` | expired unconfirmed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| education | 7 | **0** | 7 | 6 |
| government | 6 | **0** | 6 | 8 |
| public | 6 | **0** | 6 | 6 |

Every one of the 19 emitted cues carries `low_confidence: true`. **The stabilizer never reaches
its confirmation threshold on any channel**; 100% of what reaches the viewer is the
end-of-stream flush of unconfirmed hypotheses, and 20 hypotheses expired unconfirmed along the
way. This is the *unmodified* live path — it is not caused by anything I changed, and the
`array` lever I shipped feeds `observe()` bit-identical input (`identity_vs_live.array.text_and_spans_identical = 24/24`), so it neither causes nor worsens it.

INFERRED: this matches the confirmation rule at `stabilize.py:216` (`len(previous.votes) >=
stable_windows`) against the 5 s-advance / 10 s-window geometry — a token appears in at most two
consecutive windows, so reaching a `stable_windows` of more than 2 is arithmetically
impossible, and `compatible()` (`stabilize.py:153-161`) additionally refuses a match when
`window in previous.votes`, which removes the second vote. If that reading is right, the live
stabilizer is structurally incapable of committing on air, and U32's "silent caption gaps" and
this finding are the same defect seen from two ends.

**This is a product question because it changes the answer to "should we keep the 5 s
overlap?"** The brief forbids dropping the overlap because "the stabilizer needs it" — but the
measurement says the stabilizer is not using it. That premise is worth resolving before U34
spends effort making a stabilizer faster that never fires. I am not reopening it myself; it is
recorded here as an open question with its evidence.
