## Why it matters

1. **The isolation contract is broken in shipped bytes.** Every playout worker
   pulls the whole `civiccast.egress` package — 847 modules including sqlalchemy —
   into a process whose stated design goal is to need only `gi` plus seven
   siblings. That is the exact failure mode the seeding list exists to prevent.
2. **It contradicts two artifacts.** `worker.py`'s docstring says a real worker
   ends up "without `civiccast.egress`" at "321 modules"; U40's proof 4c recorded
   the worker at `reachable=11, staged=0` ("it holds nothing this time"). Both are
   wrong, and U39 had said the opposite — this is the second flip in two carries.
3. **It is a latent identity hazard, not just a cost.** The seeding exists to stop
   the engine and the worker binding two distinct `PlaylistLeg` classes
   (Gate A T4). Any *future* sibling the engine imports in package form and the
   worker forgets to publish re-opens that class of defect; `caption_flow` shows
   the list is not self-maintaining.

## Options

- **A. Publish `caption_flow` in `worker.py`** (one line: `_sibling_module("caption_flow")`
  in the load-bearing block). Restores the isolation contract, drops the worker's
  closure back toward the documented size, and removes the hazard. Cheap, but it
  is a change to live station code and needs its own carry with the usual gates —
  an engine-import-vs-published-set assertion is the natural regression test.
- **B. Leave it and correct the documentation.** Cheap, but the contract stays
  false and the next unseeded sibling re-opens the T4 class of bug.
- **C. Make it structural** — derive the publish list from the engine's
  package-form imports at import time, so the list cannot drift from the engine.

I recommend **A now, C as the follow-up**, and I am not implementing either: U42's
slice is the encoder variant, the air path, the warm bound, and the carry.
