### 2.2 (b) The control-plane job object does contain the GStreamer workers U30 relaunches

- Containment is **self-assignment, not per-child assignment**: `civiccast/app.py:2162` calls
  `_maybe_contain_own_descendants()` → `child_containment.contain_own_descendants()` →
  `api.assign_current_process(job)` (`child_containment.py:264`), guarded on `CIVICAST_SUPERVISED=1` and
  invoked at the top of `create_app`, before anything can spawn.
- **Where a relaunched worker is assigned: nowhere explicitly — it is inherited, and that is the designed
  mechanism.** `create_kill_on_job_close_job` (`child_containment.py:145-171`, the `LimitFlags` assignment

  worker spawn funnel is `_default_worker_launcher` (`civiccast/egress/gst/strategy.py:672-701`, live
  package) with `_worker_creationflags()` = `CREATE_NO_WINDOW | ABOVE_NORMAL_PRIORITY_CLASS`
  (`strategy.py:637`) — **no breakaway**. So every worker, including the one U30 relaunches, is in the
  control plane's job by inheritance from the contained control-plane process. The design note at
  `child_containment.py:41-49` says per-spawn-site assignment was deliberately rejected for exactly this
  reason.
- The only `CREATE_BREAKAWAY_FROM_JOB` in the product is
  `civiccast/native/gstreamer_repair.py:161-182` (`_detached_launch` at `:161`, the flag at `:172`, tier-5
  repair, documented to outlive its parent) — grep over `civiccast/**`, no other site.
- Why inheritance from the **supervisor's** job was not enough (and the control plane's own job is): D3's
  `native/supervisor/job_object.py` never sets `BREAKAWAY_OK`, and `KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE` fires on the last
  HANDLE close, not on member death — the supervisor holds its handle for its whole lifetime
  (`child_containment.py:18-24`).
