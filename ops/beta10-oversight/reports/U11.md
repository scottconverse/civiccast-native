## A. Caption timing/alignment — checked BEFORE changing the gate

### A.1 What assigns each cue's display time

**OBSERVED — the source of the PTS is the audio chunk's own index, on an ABSOLUTE program clock.**
`civiccast/captions/tap_worker.py:1843`:

```python
        duration = len(frames) / 2 / rate
        start = index * self._segment_seconds
```

`index` is parsed from the segment FILENAME (`chunk-NNNNNN.wav`,
`tap_worker.py:1814-1826`), and the live station's files are numbered in the tens
of thousands (`public-g0-b057791-n4` in U10's batch diagnostic → 57,791 × 5 s ≈
80 h of program clock), so this timestamp is `chunk_index * segment_seconds` and
keeps growing for the life of the station — exactly as
`civiccast/egress/gst/control.py:36-44` documents.

**OBSERVED — the display PTS is then CLAMPED to the pipeline's live edge.**
`civiccast/egress/gst/engine.py:1057-1065`:

```python
        running_time_ms = self._pipeline_running_time_ms()
        aligned_pts_ms = align_live_caption_pts_ms(
            requested_pts_ms=max(0, round(pts_seconds * 1000)),
            running_time_ms=running_time_ms,
            stream_position_ms=self._caption_stream_position_ms,
        )
        duration_ms = max(0, round(duration_seconds * 1000))
        buf.pts = aligned_pts_ms * Gst.MSECOND
```

and `civiccast/egress/gst/control.py:119-128`:

```python
    live_edge_ms = running_time_ms + lead_ms
    lead_over_edge_ms = requested_pts_ms - live_edge_ms
    if lead_over_edge_ms > max_future_lead_ms:
        return max(live_edge_ms, stream_position_ms)
    return max(requested_pts_ms, live_edge_ms, stream_position_ms)
```

`_pipeline_running_time_ms` (`engine.py:1074-1082`) is `clock.get_time() -
pipeline.get_base_time()` — i.e. it RESTARTS at each worker start.

**Therefore (INFERRED from those two OBSERVED facts, and the arithmetic is not
close):** the requested PTS is ~10⁷ ms of program clock while the pipeline's
running time is at most a few 10⁵ ms, so `lead_over_edge_ms` is far beyond
`MAX_LIVE_CAPTION_FUTURE_LEAD_MS = 8_000` (`control.py:54`) on essentially every
cue. Every live cue is rebased onto `max(live_edge, stream_position)` — the live
edge at push time.

**Answer: the display time is, in practice, wall-clock at insertion** (pipeline
running time at push + the 250 ms lead, `LIVE_CAPTION_LEAD_MS = 250`,
`control.py:33`). The audio chunk's own clock is consulted only as a
tie-breaker, and only for a cue that happens to sit within 8 s of the live edge.

### A.2 What happens to a cue whose audio is N seconds old when it is ready

**OBSERVED/INFERRED:** it is **shown**, at the live edge — i.e. **N seconds late
relative to its own audio**. It is not dropped, and it is not played at its
original airing time. `max(requested_pts_ms, live_edge_ms, stream_position_ms)`
(`control.py:128`) only ever moves a timestamp FORWARD; the two forward branches
are "old cue caught up to now" and "absurd future cue pinned to now".

**OBSERVED — nothing downstream drops a late cue either:**

- the stabilizer's only age rule is in AUDIO time, not wall time —
  `stabilize.py:501`, `oldest_relevant_end = self._latest_observed_end_seconds -
  (2 * self.window_seconds)`; the tap feeds contiguous 5 s segments whose
  hypotheses are contiguous in audio time, so a deep backlog does not by itself
  expire pending cues;
- the caption feed sends every cue it has not already sent and never expires one
  (`civiccast/egress/caption_feed.py:174-219`).

So backlog depth converts one-for-one into viewer-visible caption lateness. That
is the property the unit must not damage.

### A.3 Is there an existing latency budget constant?

**No wall-clock caption-lateness budget exists.** Every timing constant on this
path is about something else:

| Constant | Where | What it actually bounds |
|---|---|---|
| `LIVE_CAPTION_LEAD_MS = 250` | `control.py:33` | how far AHEAD of the live edge a cue is scheduled |
| `MAX_LIVE_CAPTION_FUTURE_LEAD_MS = 8_000` | `control.py:54` | a clock-epoch guard, and its docstring only *observes* that "Real ASR/first-buffer latency is sub-second to low single-digit seconds" (`control.py:51-53`) — it does not cap lateness |
| `2 * window_seconds` (8 s) | `stabilize.py:501` | pending-cue expiry in AUDIO time |
| `capture_seconds = 6.0` | `caption_proof_worker.py:258` | how much emitted stream the decode-back PROOF samples |
| `timing_tolerance_seconds = 0.75` | `caption_embed.py:132` | proof match tolerance |

### A.4 The lateness cost of raising `max_backlog_segments`, and the decision

**Arithmetic:** a tolerated backlog of K segments is K × 5 s of audio that is
transcribed that much later, and by A.2 each cue is displayed when it is pushed.
Raising the limit from 2 to K therefore adds **up to (K − 2) × 5 s of
viewer-visible caption lateness**: K=6 → +20 s, K=10 → +40 s, K=15 → +65 s.

**Decision: `max_backlog_segments` is NOT raised.** No constant in the code
permits the extra lateness, the goal statement forbids captions arriving later
relative to their audio, and raising it would not address the measured defect
anyway (U10: 70% of the 182 trips fire at exactly 3 against a max of 2 — a
higher limit merely moves the trip to a new minimal overshoot, and under a
sustained slow-ASR window it makes captions progressively later rather than
fixing anything). **B2 (persistence) is the whole fix; B3 is not applied.**

What B2 costs on the same axis, stated plainly: the pause is deferred by up to
the persistence window (K scans × 2 s, see B.2), so under a *genuine* collapse
the tap keeps transcribing for up to that window before shedding, instead of
dropping at the first over-limit scan. In the transient case B2's effect on
lateness is strictly positive: today the over-limit segments are **deleted with
no caption at all** and the channel blanks for 120 s; under B2 they are
transcribed and shown a few seconds late. B2 also keeps the ASR call size
unchanged (batches stay capped at `max_backlog_segments`, see B.2) so the
deferral never issues a larger ASR call than the station issues today.

## B. Implementation

### B.1 Discard the previous broadcast's audio at worker construction

`CaptionTapWorker.__init__` now calls `_discard_pre_existing_segments()`
(`tap_worker.py:1500-1541`) — a new `for channel_dir in self._tap_root.iterdir()`
sweep that calls the existing `_discard_settled_segments(channel_id,
context="worker startup")` for each channel directory. **OBSERVED** the
construction call site, immediately after the sidecar reset it mirrors:

```
civiccast/captions/tap_worker.py:639        self._discard_pre_existing_segments()
```

`_discard_settled_segments` gained a keyword-only `context` used only in the log
line ("session start" for the explicit START hook, "worker startup" here). It
uses the RAW numbered list, newest file included — deliberately, not
`_settled_segments`: at a session boundary there is no live writer for this
session yet, so every chunk present belongs to the finished broadcast (the
pre-existing comment says exactly that, `tap_worker.py:1557-1561`).

**Why a construction-time watermark is sound and mtime is not:** every chunk
present at construction was produced by something other than this worker (a
previous process, or a session that has ended), so no writer's ownership has to
be inferred from the filesystem. The repo's own SCOPE LIMIT note on
`_begin_channel_session_locked` records why file mtime cannot establish that,
and that reasoning still holds — which is why this is not an mtime staleness
window.

**Scope:** only what exists at construction. A segment the tap writes afterwards
is this session's audio and is processed normally, even before the first scan
(`test_segments_written_after_construction_are_processed`).

**Not fatal:** an unreadable tap root logs at DEBUG with `exc_info` and returns
0. A worker that could not be constructed would take the control plane — and the
station — down over a caption-tap cleanup.

**Cost, stated plainly:** a control-plane restart in the middle of a broadcast
now throws away whatever was sitting in each channel's tap directory at that
moment (at most `max_backlog_segments + 1` segments, ~15 s of audio at the
shipped settings) instead of transcribing it. That is the deliberate trade: the
alternative is publishing captions for audio that aired minutes ago, displayed
at the live edge (section A.2), and — per the deferral below — counting it as
live backlog until the persistence window expires and pausing on it anyway.

### B.2 Pause only on a PERSISTENT overshoot

New module constant and setting (`tap_worker.py:162`, `:300`, `:343`, `:498`),
wired through `build_tap_worker` (`:2368`):

```python
DEFAULT_OVERLOAD_PERSISTENCE_SCANS = 15
```

`from_env` reads `CIVICCAST_CAPTION_TAP_OVERLOAD_PERSISTENCE_SCANS` through the
same `_env_int` helper as the backlog bound, which raises on anything below 1 —
so `0` is fatal at settings level and the constructor additionally raises
`ValueError` for a directly constructed worker with a value below 1.

Scan-loop behaviour (`tap_worker.py:1100-1200`), in evaluation order:

1. **No settled segments at all** → clear the streak and skip. This is a change
   from the pre-U11 loop, which `continue`d before reaching any counter: a scan
   with nothing queued is under the limit, and the streak has to be
   *consecutive*, so a healthy gap clears it rather than merely failing to
   advance it. A genuinely behind channel never reaches this branch, because the
   deferral leaves the excess queued and the settle guard leaves the newest file.
2. **A batch is in flight** → count the depth (`_note_backlog_depth`) and skip.
   Mid-batch scans are counted because a slow batch *is* the measured shape of
   the defect (p90 5.7 s, max 19.08 s); the trip itself is only ever taken on an
   idle scan.
3. **Nothing queued** (everything settled is in flight) → clear and skip.
4. **Paused** → clear the streak (a draining channel must not carry a streak into
   the scan that releases it) and drain, unchanged.
5. **Over the limit** → `_note_backlog_depth` returns False until the 15th
   consecutive over-limit scan. Until then:
   - the oldest `max_backlog_segments` segments of that scan are submitted as a
     normal batch, so **the ASR call size is exactly a legal batch's** (the unit
     must not turn one over-limit scan into a six-segment call on the box the
     pause exists to protect);
   - the excess stays queued for the next scan instead of being deleted;
   - one INFO line per episode (`_announce_deferred_backlog`, `tap_worker.py:847`),
     deliberately not the overload line: `"Caption tap is behind for channel %s:
     %d settled segments is over the maximum %d on scan %d of %d. Transcribing
     the oldest %d now and leaving the rest queued; live captions continue."`
   On the 15th, the pre-existing fail-closed path runs **unchanged** — same
   `_record_discarded_batch`, same `_invalidate_inflight_for_overload`, same
   `_fail_closed_overload` (sidecar cleared, audio discarded, 120 s first pause,
   ladder escalation) — and the streak is then reset, because the episode is over.
6. **At or under the limit** → clear the streak, legal batch, unchanged.
7. The streak is also cleared in `_begin_channel_session_locked`, in both
   `_run_disabled` loops, and on a pause — every place the backoff state itself
   is forgotten.

**Deferred scans are not recovery evidence.** The pending tuple carries a
`deferred` flag (now the 6th element, `tap_worker.py:1087-1199`), threaded
through `_submit_channel` → `_isolated_process_channel` →
`_process_channel(deferred_over_limit=...)`. When set, the completion path
publishes the unchanged `within-capacity` status but **skips
`record_within_capacity`** (`tap_worker.py:1459-1481`). Without that, a station
still behind would walk its escalation ladder back down during the deferral and
then trip again at the BASE window — the exact flap the ladder exists to
prevent. `test_an_over_limit_scan_is_not_recovery_evidence_for_the_ladder` pins
it with a one-scan recovery window: if the deferrals counted, the second trip
would open at 60 s instead of 120 s.

### K = 15, and why not the brief's example of 10

**OBSERVED (from U10, the basis the brief supplied):** 182 trips in a day, 70% at
exactly 3 settled segments against a max of 2; per-batch ASR elapsed p50 1.95 s,
p90 5.73 s, **max 19.08 s**; ASR pool idle with no lock waits at trip time.

**Arithmetic:** the transient behind a worst-case batch is a ~19.08 s stretch
with the queue over the limit — ~9.5 scans at the shipped 2 s poll. K = 15 is
15 × 2 s = **30 s = 1.57× that tail**, while still shedding a genuine collapse
in a quarter of the 120 s first pause. K = 10 (the brief's 20 s example) would be
1.05× the measured worst tail — a *single* measured batch would sit exactly on
the boundary, which is not a margin at all. The brief's "e.g." is illustrative
("K such that K × 2 s ≥ 20 s"); 15 satisfies it and is the one with real
headroom over the data it is derived from.

**EPISTEMIC LIMIT — stated in the code comment too, so the number is not
overclaimed:** the field log cannot measure how long an over-limit episode would
have lasted unfixed, because **every trip today truncates the episode** (it drops
the audio and pauses). 19.08 s is the longest batch that was *observed to
complete*, not the longest transient that existed. K = 15 is therefore a bounded
engineering judgement on a measured tail, not a measured distribution.

`overload_persistence_scans=1` restores the pre-U11 single-scan rule exactly and
is documented as the operator's escape hatch.

  else in the unit turned on a product decision: B4's boundary was respected, and
  the only new knob (`overload_persistence_scans`, default 15) is a threshold
  with a documented escape hatch rather than a new capability.

### Doubts

1. **Episode length is unmeasurable from today's log** (the truncation point
   above). If real transients regularly exceed 30 s, K=15 is too small and the
   false alarms return — just later. The counter-evidence is that the measured
   tail is 19.08 s and that 70% of trips fired at the *minimum possible*
   overshoot, which is the signature of a one-scan artifact, not of a long
   episode. A cheap future check: log the depth on every over-limit scan and
   measure episode lengths against the new gate.
2. **Deferred-path pile growth under a real collapse.** For 30 s the tap keeps
   transcribing 2 segments per scan while ~2 new arrive — net queue growth. The
   pause then deletes the pile as it does today, so this is bounded by the window,
   but it is a new 30 s of disk growth on a station already behind. A disk-full
   station during a collapse would notice.
3. **A permanently-in-flight channel delays the trip.** The depth is counted
   mid-batch but the trip is only taken on an idle scan. Under a collapse where
   batches never leave the channel idle, the pause is delayed (not prevented).
   I judged that acceptable, not ideal.
4. **The deferred scan's status surface is unchanged** — it publishes
   `within-capacity` with `backlog_segments` set to the *batch* size, not the true
   queue depth. An operator watching `runtime-status.json` cannot see the tap
   being behind; only the log and the opt-in batch diagnostic show it. Adding a
   state would be a product decision (B4's boundary), so it is reported, not
   done.
5. **B1 discards ~15 s of live audio on a mid-broadcast restart** (cost above).
   Defensible, but it is a real loss of captions at the exact moment a station is
   restarted, and a future unit may want the START hook's session boundary to be
   the only place that discards.
6. **`overload_persistence_scans=1` in the batch-diagnostic module** means the
   shipped default is not exercised by that module any more. Intentional (the
   shipped window is covered thoroughly in `test_caption_tap_worker.py`), but a
   reader of that module alone would not know the gate's real default.
