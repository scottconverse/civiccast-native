## 1. The answer in plain English

While a channel is on air, the live caption tap listens to the program audio and
transcribes it in small batches. It is a soft-real-time job: each ~5-second batch
of audio should be transcribed in well under the 5 seconds it takes to air. When
it cannot keep up, its queue grows; when the queue grows past the bound, the tap
sheds the oldest batches and **discards that spoken audio**. The captions for
those seconds never appear. On 2026-10-01 that happened 13 times in 8 hours,
throwing away between 10 and 40 seconds of speech each time.

Two things are true about why, and only one of them is fixable by the change in
this slice.

**First — the structural half, and the half this slice fixes.** The tap's own
transcribe thread is explicitly dropped to `BELOW_NORMAL` priority
(`civiccast.captions.tap_worker`). The conform ffmpeg that a **foreground** —
that is, on-air, synchronous — preparation spawns did not use that thread. It
started at the service process's own priority: **`NORMAL`**, with its encoder
capped at `_foreground_thread_cap()` (8 on this box) and its **decoder and filter
graph not capped at all**. So during a cold preparation the tap and the conform
were two parties asking the same box for the same cores, and only one of them had
been told to yield — the tap. The other one, by construction, was the only party
in that tie that could win it.

That is a real, one-line-shaped defect, and it is what this slice fixes: every
ffmpeg child the foreground preparation spawns now starts one scheduling class
down (Windows `BELOW_NORMAL_PRIORITY_CLASS`, POSIX `nice -n 10`).

**Second — the half this slice does *not* fix, and says so.** The correlation I
expected is much weaker than the brief's lead hypothesis implies. Of the 87 shed
events retained in the logs, only **47 (54 %)** have any preparation in flight in
the 30 seconds before the shed, against a wall-clock preparation occupancy of
17.6 %. **46 % of the sheds — including the deepest single event, 100 seconds on
2026-10-01 at 16:21:35 — happen with no preparation running at all.** The whole
18:40:09–18:43:21 cluster of seven small sheds has no preparation in flight
either; that cluster lines up with disk activity, not with a conform. In the
brief's own 13-event window, only 5 of 13 have a prep in flight.

So: the tap loses to a foreground conform **when a conform is running**, and this
change removes that specific asymmetry. It cannot be the whole story, and the
report says so wherever it could be read otherwise (§5, §8).

## 2. What the evidence settles, and what it does not

Everything below comes from the tap's own instruments and the station's retained
logs. Where an instrument is blind, that is stated rather than papered over.

**Settled — the delay is inside the ASR call, not in feed or queue.**
The tap's batch records carry both `age_seconds` (how old the batch was when it
was pulled) and `elapsed_seconds` (how long the transcribe call took), plus
`queue_depth`. On essentially every long record `age_seconds == elapsed_seconds`
with `queue_depth == 1`. That is the signature of a *synchronous* stall: the
worker pulled a batch the moment it became available (nothing was waiting behind
it) and then sat inside the transcribe call. It rules out "the feeder thread was
starved and the queue backed up" for the long batches — the queue is depth 1, not
depth 25.

Batch transcribe times: **max 21.157 s, p50 0.907 s, p90 3.656 s, p99 8.688 s.**
A 5-second segment taking 21 seconds to transcribe is a 4.2× overshoot; at p90 the
tap is at 0.73× realtime and losing ground on every batch.

**Settled — the tap is chronically one segment over its bound, not flooding.**
Of 12,483 over-limit scans, **9,301 (74.5 %) are at depth 3** — the smallest
possible over-limit state when the bound is 2. The largest recorded depth is 25
(2026-09-27 22:25:23, education, 115 s discarded). This matters for the fix
choice: the tap is not drowning, it is *just barely* behind, repeatedly. A modest
scheduling change is the right size of instrument; a large architectural change
would be treating a symptom that is one unit deep 74.5 % of the time.

**Settled — the code path confirms the priority asymmetry.** Read from the live
bytes: the foreground conform is launched from a `NORMAL`-priority
`egress-prepare-N` thread, `run_ffmpeg` is called without `lower_priority`, so no
`BELOW_NORMAL_PRIORITY_CLASS` creationflag is applied; the tap's ASR thread is
explicitly `_THREAD_PRIORITY_BELOW_NORMAL = -1`. The CTranslate2 intra-op pool
inherits the *process* priority (`NORMAL`) and **is not reachable from Python** —
this is documented in `tap_worker.py` itself, and it is the reason fix candidate
(a) is nearly inert (§4).

**Not settled — why the 40 prep-free sheds happen.** This is the honest gap.
A prep-free shed means the tap lost to *something else*: another process, disk
contention, a scheduler hiccup, or the CTranslate2 pool contending with itself.
The retained logs cannot separate those, because **both of the tap's diagnostics
are blind to the shed windows**: `phase_timing` self-exhausts
(`_MAX_EVENTS=4000`, `_MAX_SECONDS=600.0`) and `tap_batch_diagnostic` is a 900 s
window held as 13 contiguous ~15-minute runs, one per tap-worker start, all
marked `exhausted=true`. **63 of the 87 sheds fall outside any instrumented
window.** For those windows I have the shed record and nothing else.

**The one log line that would settle it.** An unbounded, per-shed diagnostic
emitted at the moment the over-limit streak starts (and again when the shed
fires), recording the four phase timings — queue-wait, decode/feed, ASR
inference, stabilise — plus process and thread state (CPU seconds burned by the
process over the window, its thread count, and whether the transcribe thread was
runnable-but-unscheduled versus blocked). That single line separates the three
candidate mechanisms: waiting on inference (GPU/CPU-bound), waiting on audio-chunk
extraction (disk-bound), and the worker thread never being scheduled (starvation).
It is **not** in this slice — the brief asked for the smallest fix, and a new tap
instrument is a different change with its own review. It is named as follow-up 1
in §8.

## 3. Root cause, walked against the running code

1. `civiccast/egress/preparer.py:_prepare_segment` runs on the **air path** — the
   channel is on air and the next segment is needed *now*. It is the only conform
   that runs while the caption tap is transcribing; a warm conform runs when
   nothing is on air, which is why `lower_priority=True` was already the right
   default for it (U60).
2. That call computed `conform_threads = 1 if lower_priority else _foreground_thread_cap()`.
   On the air path `lower_priority` is `False`, so it got `_foreground_thread_cap()`
   = `max(1, cpu_count // 2)` = **8**. That is correct and is deliberately
   unchanged — pinning the foreground path to the warm's single thread is measured
   at **6.4× slower (233 s vs 36.6 s for 300 s of content, `_foreground_thread_cap`'s
   own docstring)** and could push a preparation past the channel's 690 s lead.
3. The same `lower_priority` flag was also the *only* thing driving process
   priority. `False` on the air path meant `run_ffmpeg` applied no creationflags,
   so the child inherited `NORMAL`.
4. `run_ffmpeg` (`civiccast/stream/_ffmpeg.py:511`) already knows how to demote a
   child — Windows `BELOW_NORMAL_PRIORITY_CLASS` via `getattr(subprocess, …)`, POSIX
   a `nice -n 10` argv prefix — and it is used by the warm path. Its docstring says
   the demotion "must never become a blanket default here". This change does not
   touch that default; it only supplies the flag on one more call path.
5. Net effect before the fix: the tap (dropped to below-normal by its own code) and
   the foreground conform (inheriting normal) met on unequal terms chosen by the
   code, not by the operator.

**The fix, in one sentence.** Decouple priority from thread count on the foreground
path: keep `conform_threads = _foreground_thread_cap()` and set the child's
priority one class down by default.

## 4. Which candidate, and why not the others

The brief named three candidates. I did **one** — candidate (b), priority only,
bounded — and here is why the others were rejected on evidence rather than taste.

- **(a) Raise per-channel ASR/feed threads to `NORMAL`, leave playout alone —
  rejected.** Read from the live bytes: the only Python thread the tap demotes is
  the ASR **dispatch** thread. The work happens in the CTranslate2 intra-op pool,
  which inherits the *process* priority and is not reachable from Python — this is
  stated in `tap_worker.py`'s own comments. Raising the dispatch thread would
  therefore be nearly inert for the mechanism in question, while adding scheduling
  risk to the live encoder and playout, which is a much larger blast radius for no
  measured gain.
- **(c) Both, bounded — rejected.** It would inherit (a)'s unjustified risk, and
  the brief explicitly said not to do all of them.
- **(b) Demote the foreground preparation's ffmpeg children — chosen.** It is the
  only candidate that removes a *code-chosen* asymmetry between two parties that
  the system put in direct competition, and it changes only *when* ffmpeg's threads
  run, never what they compute (§6 proves the last clause byte-for-byte).

**Deliberately not coupled to the thread count.** U60 tied `lower_priority` to
*both* the priority and a single-thread cap, because a warm wants both. An on-air
conform does not: it wants to yield the CPU but it must still finish inside the
channel's lead. So the new local is a second, independent knob — `conform_threads`
still comes from `lower_priority` exactly as before, and the new boolean
`conform_lower_priority = lower_priority or _foreground_preparation_low_priority()`
drives only the four `run_ffmpeg` calls. Read once per call, like `conform_threads`,
so all four ffmpeg stages a preparation runs make the same decision.

**The knob.** `CIVICAST_EGRESS_PREPARE_LOW_PRIORITY`, **default ON**.

| input | result |
|---|---|
| unset / empty | ON (the default) |
| `1` `true` `yes` `on` (any case, surrounding whitespace ignored) | ON |
| `0` `false` `no` `off` | OFF — restores the pre-U68 behavior exactly |
| anything else | WARNING **once per distinct value** (latched), default kept |

Two C's, matching the spelling the station's service registry writes; the one-C
spelling is inert (`civiccast/egress/env_vars.py`, the BETA.10 U03 trap). An
unrecognized value never silently means "off" and never floods a log that a
preparation writes to many times an hour — both of those are the failure modes a
priority knob must not have, because its wrong answer is invisible until captions
go missing.

## 5. Worst-case effect on a cold preparation (work item 3)

**What I measured.** The preparer's own `build_conform_source_args`, at
`_foreground_thread_cap()` = 8 threads, one ffmpeg at a time, on a source built
from real station media: the first 20 segments of
`fixtures/C12-government-0643` (each ~2.0 s, 1280x720 h264 30 fps) concatenated at
the file level — **21,444,784 bytes, 39.999989 s** of content.

| arm | runs (s) | median |
|---|---|---|
| `NORMAL` | 1.916 / 1.768 / 1.910 | 1.910 |
| `BELOW_NORMAL` | 1.836 / 1.765 / 1.856 | 1.836 |

Pooling the warm-up runs as well: 1.664–1.916 vs 1.765–1.877. **Delta −0.074 s
(−3.87 %) at the median — inside the ±8 % run-to-run spread of the bench itself.**

Box state during the measurement: ~90 % CPU, three ffmpeg processes running under
the service account, a `BelowNormal` python process holding 809 s of CPU. That is
not a quiet box, which is the point — this is a *contended* measurement, not an
idle one.

**Why the demotion cannot cost much here.** `ffmpeg -benchmark` on the identical
conform:

| arm | utime | stime | rtime | CPU-seconds | cores in flight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `NORMAL` | 10.469 | 1.719 | 1.664 | 12.188 | **7.32** |
| `BELOW_NORMAL` | 11.734 | 1.516 | 1.877 | 13.250 | **7.06** |

The conform demands ~7 cores in flight. A priority class bounds *latency under
contention*; it does not reduce a job's *throughput* when the cores are there. On
a 16-core box the conform wants 7, so it is not the oversubscription by itself —
it is the oversubscription *on top of* the tap, the live encoder and playout.

**Fixed cost.** A 2 s source takes rtime 0.228 s; the 40 s source takes 1.757 s. The
fixed per-conform startup (process spawn, demuxer/decoder open, filter-graph init,
hardware-encoder session init) is therefore **≈ 0.148 s** — negligible, and *not*
a burst that lands on the tap at the start of every preparation. The cost is
proportional to content.

**Extrapolation against the lead.** The longest observed preparation is **389 s
against a ~690 s lead**. The station's own numbers put the conform at ~18.5×
realtime, matching this bench (~21×), so there is no sustained-throughput problem
to find and the bench is representative. An 8 % spread on 389 s is ±31 s → 420 s,
**still ~270 s inside the lead** — and the change's own contribution is *inside*
that band, not additive to it, because the two arms overlap.

**What could NOT be measured, and why.**
- **A true worst-case oversubscription arm** — two concurrent heavy jobs — is
  forbidden by the brief's "one heavy job at a time" and by the station being on
  air. The 90 %-CPU box above is the closest honest proxy.
- **Whether the tap's transcribe thread is actually runnable-but-starved at the
  instant of a real shed.** That needs the per-shed diagnostic in §2, which is
  follow-up 1. Until it exists, the causal claim "the demotion will stop these
  sheds" is *inferred from a code structure plus a timeline correlation*, not
  observed at the shed instant. It is stated here rather than buried.
- **The station's own ffmpeg.** The bench used the ffmpeg on this machine's PATH
  (`…Gyan.FFmpeg.Essentials…ffmpeg-8.1.1-essentials_build\bin\ffmpeg.EXE`), which
  has libx264 **and** h264_nvenc. The station's build is `--disable-libx264` by
  policy. The encoder the preparer actually resolved in the measured runs was
  **`h264_mf`** — the hardware encoder, present in both builds and the same choice
  the station would make — but a reader should know which binary was on PATH.
  `-threads 8` was confirmed present in the measured argv.

## 6. No output change, structurally and empirically

**Structurally.** The complete `git diff civiccast/egress/preparer.py` against the
live bytes is exactly three things: (a) one env-parsing helper and its two module
constants, (b) one boolean local, (c) four `lower_priority=lower_priority,` →
`lower_priority=conform_lower_priority,` substitutions. **No** ffmpeg argument
list, filter graph, encoder setting, loudness target, conform decision, caption
string, cache key or scheduling decision is touched. Grep confirms the warm/cache
call sites (lines 913, 1038, 1234, 1247, 1297, incl. the explicit
`lower_priority=True` at 1787/1985) and `_run_ffmpeg`'s internal pass-through
(2525) are untouched.

**Empirically.** All six measured runs across both priorities produced the
**identical output: 17,039,372 bytes, sha256
`4bddc44f073960fd844521590652997900c5afbf3e377a3e31216dd711e257e2`**, with
`byte_identical_across_priorities: true`. Which is what the mechanism predicts: a
scheduling class changes *when* a process's threads run, not what they compute.

Caption TEXT is untouched by construction — the change is in `egress/preparer.py`
and the diff contains no captions module, no model, no prompt and no transcript
path.

## 9. Risks, and what is deliberately left out

1. **This fix cannot be the whole story.** 46 % of the retained sheds — including
   the deepest, 100 s on 2026-10-01 — have **no preparation in flight** in the 30 s
   before the shed. Expect the shed count to fall, not to reach zero. Anyone reading
   a post-install shed count should read it against that baseline, not against zero.
2. **The causal claim is inferred, not observed at the shed instant.** The code
   structure is read from the live bytes; the timeline correlation is 54 % with a
   17.6 % base rate. Neither is the same as watching the tap at the moment it loses.
   Follow-up 1 below is what would close this.
3. **Cannot be measured on an on-air station.** No concurrent-heavy-job arm (§5).
   The ±8 % band is the bench's own spread, not a station measurement.
4. **The bench's ffmpeg is not the station's** (Gyan winget build, libx264 present;
   encoder resolved to `h264_mf` in the runs). The station is `--disable-libx264` by
   policy. The priority mechanism is build-independent; the wall-clock numbers are
   from whichever binary was on PATH.
5. **The on-air conform now yields to *everything* at normal priority, not just the
   tap.** That is the intended trade: latency under contention in exchange for the
   tap keeping up. A preparation that finishes slightly later but inside its 690 s
   lead is the correct outcome; a preparation that finishes late is a channel
   outage. §5's extrapolation (~270 s of headroom) is why I judge this safe, and it
   is the number to watch after install.
6. **`_foreground_thread_cap()` is unchanged at 8.** If post-install measurements
   show preparations drifting toward the lead, the next lever is threading (the
   decoder and `-vf` graph are still uncapped), not priority — and it should be
   driven by a station measurement, not by another guess.

**Named follow-ups, not fixed here (the brief asked for the smallest fix):**

1. **Per-shed diagnostic** — an unbounded line emitted when the over-limit streak
   starts, recording queue-wait / decode / ASR-inference / stabilise and thread
   state. This is the single change that would turn §2's "not settled" into
   settled, and it is the highest-value next slice on this thread.
2. `check_streaming_loudness` still runs its own ffmpeg at `NORMAL` priority
   (bounded window, `-vn`, `-threads` correctly placed *before* `-i`).
3. `-threads N` is still emitted **after** `-i` in `build_conform_source_args`, so it
   caps the encoder only and leaves the decoder and filter graph uncapped. (In
   `build_loudnorm_probe_args` the placement is already correct.)
