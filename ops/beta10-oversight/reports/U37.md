### 1.2 Why the mux stops emitting video — and why the whole tail then carries one frozen DTS (OBSERVED, with one INFERRED step)

A deferred program→program switch rebases the incoming leg by giving its sticky SEGMENT a new base
(`set_offset` on the leg's own tail src pads). On the mux side (`gst_aggregator_default_sink_event` in
the GStreamer sources read off-live during this slice): the rebased segment arrives as an ordinary event
and is copied into `agg_pad->segment` **the moment it arrives** — but the buffers already queued on that
pad are clipped **later, at POP time**, under whatever segment is current *then*. So one outgoing-leg
buffer still sitting in the video pad's queue when the rebased segment lands is re-dated into the new
base's timeline: its running time becomes its own running time **plus this transaction's rebase offset**.

The recorded numbers fit exactly. `x4dbg/run3`'s own reload lines (`raw/logs/base-x4dbg-run3-stages.txt`,
quoted from that run's worker log) report both halves:

## 3. The fix (brief item 2)

A **bounded, lossless drain gate** on the deferred-switch commit path. Nothing in the switch order
changes: the gate runs strictly *before* the first selector mutation, so the outgoing/incoming handover
order the U30/U34 tests pin is untouched. Symbols (`civiccast/egress/gst/engine.py`): family at
:5310-5600, block comment :5263-5308, constants :570-575.

- `_rebase_affected_mux_pads` (:5310) resolves the transaction's OWN pads (`new_video_pad` /
  `new_audio_pad`) to mux sink pads by stream label — not `_required_stream_labels()`, because a stream
  the new leg does not carry is not switched and must not be waited on.
- `_rebase_drain_held` (:5468) reads `current-level-buffers` on each affected pad: the aggregator's own
  queue depth, which it decrements as it **pops** a buffer (clipped buffer included), so zero means the
  pad has given up everything it took before the rebase. **An unreadable level is reported `unknown` and
  is therefore not proven empty** — treating it as empty would silently reinstate the re-dating.
- `_defer_rebase_drain` (:5492) hands the commit to a main-loop poller
  (`_REBASE_DRAIN_POLL_MS = 20 ms`, :575) rather than blocking, so the stall watchdog, commit watchdog
  and control-plane reader stay alive on a channel that is otherwise still on air.
- `_resume_rebase_drain` (:5522) switches once the pads are empty, or at
  `_REBASE_DRAIN_DEADLINE_S = 3.0 s` (:570) with a WARN naming the pad it could not drain. If no poller
  can be scheduled it switches now with a WARN — never waits forever.
- Lossless, deliberately: those queued buffers are ordinary outgoing-leg media and leave by being
  **consumed**, not dropped. The first draft's DROP-until-SEGMENT fence was pure harm — measured
  (`raw/logs/fence-variant-x2-run3.txt`) as `stage=rebase-fence-disarmed pad=sink_66 stream=audio
  arrived=47` and, on the reference capture, `dropped=48`, with that capture airing **1013** audio frames
  against the 1061 every non-dropping run airs (a 1.024 s hole) and 653 video frames against 659. So the
  arrival probe is an **observer** that counts and changes nothing (`_arm_rebase_segment_observers`
  :5352, `_make_rebase_segment_observer` :5424, `_on_rebase_observer_deadline` :5561,
  `_release_rebase_observers` :5582, `_REBASE_OBSERVER_DEADLINE_S = 1.0 s` :571).

## 4. The watchdog (brief item 3)

`_check_stream_stalls` (:2976) no longer reads arrival counts. It reads each mux sink pad's **airing
running time** — the running time the pad's `buffer-consumed` recorder reports for buffers the mux
actually emitted (`_install_mux_pad_airing_frontier` :2031, `_make_mux_pad_consumed_recorder` :2059) —
and judges two predicates with **one action** (`_act_on_stream_stall` :3164):

- **flat** — a required stream whose airing running time does not advance with wall clock for
  `stall_timeout_s`. Consulted **first**; it owns the attribution, because a frozen stream's lag against
  the leader also grows without bound, so a lag-only judge would name the audio the frozen video left
  behind instead of the video. Its printed wording is preserved byte-for-byte.
- **lag** — a stream still advancing but sitting more than `_STREAM_LAG_BOUND_S = 2.0 s` (:605) behind
  the leading required stream's airing running time, continuously, for that same budget (default
  `stall_timeout_s = 10.0 s`).

Both route through `_act_on_stream_stall`, which is **U34's action unchanged**: force a ready deferred
switch through if one is held, else exit non-zero naming the stream.

### 4.2 Why the flat half is not enough on its own (OBSERVED)

The live 15:55 incident is *not* a flat case: the coordinator's note records the post-commit mux-in counts
as steady and healthy (`video=+150 audio=+235` per 5 s) while the src total advanced audio-only. A
flat-only judge sees a frozen frontier and fires; the lag half covers the case where the stream never
fully stops but is left seconds behind. Both are in, and the new test
`test_per_stream_stall_judges_a_stream_that_keeps_advancing_but_runs_far_behind` drives exactly that

## 8. Doubts and known gaps

- **The red case is a race, and its rate is not a property of the offset.** 4 of 21 base-engine runs
  collapsed, and the same `ends=` shapes that collapsed also aired clean (§1.3); the live evidence adds
  a third data point (4 of the last 5 such boundaries froze, then 17:34 did not). I am claiming
  "reproduced, at a measurable rate, on the real media", not "reproduces every time".
- **The green is 15 runs.** 0/15 is consistent with the mechanism, and the drain diagnostics appear in
  all 15, but 15 runs cannot bound a race to zero; the base rate is ~19%, so 15 clean runs is
  suggestive, not proof.
- **The mpegtsmux-internal step is INFERRED.** I did not take a `GST_DEBUG=mpegtsmux:6,aggregator:6`
  capture, so the *aggregator's* reason for holding the video pad out of the interleave — and for
  stamping one frozen DTS on the tail — is read off the recorded output and the GStreamer sources, not
  observed in a trace. What is observed: the re-dated frame, the backstep, the frozen-DTS law, and the
  478-emission audio-only run.
- **The per-run engine identity gap.** Harness stdout with its resolved `engine_src` sha256 was captured
  for one base batch only (`raw/harness/x4base.txt`). For every other run, base-vs-candidate is carried
  by the behavioural markers in `evidence/README.md` (the U37 stages exist only in the candidate engine)
  plus the pinned base-tree hash. The captured trees are deleted, so this cannot be re-derived from here.
- **The candidate's healthy lag is 4.9× the base engine's.** Candidate worst healthy 0.726678 s against
  base-engine worst healthy 0.149322 s. Both are far below the 2.0 s bound, but the difference is
  unexplained — the builds differ only in the drain gate and the judge — and it is the reason I state
  the bound's margin as 2.75× (against the candidate's own worst healthy) rather than against the tighter
  base figure.
- **The bound is chosen from off-live runs.** All 74 healthy rows are off-live; a station under a
  different load could present a wider healthy lag. 2.75×/4.70× is the measured headroom, not a
  guarantee.
