## 1. Item 1 — the warm path, with `file:line`

All references are `civiccast/egress/preparer.py` unless stated. **OBSERVED** by reading the
committed source at `ed5c8464`.

**Trigger.** The air path `_prepare_segment` reaches its cache-MISS branch and calls
`self._schedule_warm(...)` at **`preparer.py:2442`** — for a trimmed miss, a slot-capped (D42)
untrimmed conform that covers only a fragment of the asset, or a whole-asset window whose
loudnorm measurement degraded to single-pass (U20). It passes `media_duration_seconds=media_duration`,
which is the number the new timeout scaling keys off.

**Queue and worker.** `_schedule_warm` (definition `preparer.py:1294`) hands a closure to
`self._warm_scheduler` (`preparer.py:1439`), the production default being
`_default_warm_scheduler` (`preparer.py:240`). That puts the job on a module-level FIFO
`_warm_queue`, drained by **exactly one** long-lived daemon thread named
`conform-cache-warm` (`preparer.py:221` `_warm_worker`, started once at `preparer.py:256`
behind `_warm_worker_started`). One background whole-asset conform at a time, process-wide.

**Dedupe and locking.** `self._warming: set[str]` (`preparer.py:468`) under
`self._warming_guard` (`preparer.py:467`) keeps the same cache key from being queued twice
while its warm is pending; the key is added inside the guard (`preparer.py:1352`) and always
discarded in the job's `finally` (`preparer.py:1439`-region). **`_schedule_cache_copy_promotion`
(`preparer.py:1053`) shares both the same worker and the same `_warming` set** — a second job
type on the one serial queue.

**Work and its ffmpeg calls.** The job re-checks the cache (it may have been populated while
queued), then calls `_conform_full_asset_into_cache` (`preparer.py:725`), which calls
`_measure_loudnorm_metadata` (`preparer.py:640`) for the loudnorm pass-1 probe and then the
pass-2 conform. **Every one of those ffmpeg invocations goes through `self._run_ffmpeg`
(`preparer.py:1757`)**.

**Where the flat bound was applied.** `_DEFAULT_PREPARATION_TIMEOUT_SECONDS = 300.0`
(`preparer.py:73`), sourced by `preparation_timeout_seconds_from_env()` (`preparer.py:282`)
into `self._preparation_timeout_seconds`, and applied unconditionally by the old `_run_ffmpeg`
to *every* caller. That is the defect: the foreground path's unit of work is a bounded
segment, the warm's is the whole asset — one bound for both made every long meeting
unwarmable. **INFERRED** from the code, and confirmed by the symptom (§6).

**The on-demand path, for contrast.** `_prepare_segment` builds and runs its own conform
synchronously on the caller's thread; it acquires no `self._conform_locks` entry for this and
never sits on `_warm_queue`. The only cross-talk with a warm is CPU. §3.4.

## 3. Item 3 — the four fixes

### 3.1 (3a) The warm's timeout scales with the asset

New public helper, `preparer.py:326`:

```python
estimate = (
    media_duration_seconds / _WARM_CONFORM_DESIGN_SPEED_RPS * _WARM_CONFORM_TIMEOUT_FACTOR
)
return max(base_timeout_seconds, min(estimate, _WARM_CONFORM_TIMEOUT_CEILING_SECONDS))
```

with `base_timeout_seconds` returned unchanged when the duration is `None` or non-positive —
so the base is a **floor**, and the change can only ever widen a warm's budget.

Computed once in `_schedule_warm` at **`preparer.py:1337`**, *outside* the job closure, so the
number handed to ffmpeg and the number the failure warning prints are provably the same value.

**How it reaches ffmpeg without touching the air path.** `_run_ffmpeg` (`preparer.py:1757`)
gained two keyword-only parameters, `timeout_seconds: float | None = None` and
`lower_priority: bool = False`; `None` means "use `self._preparation_timeout_seconds`", i.e.
exactly the old behavior. `_measure_loudnorm_metadata` (`preparer.py:645-648`) and
`_conform_full_asset_into_cache` (`preparer.py:733-736`) each gained the same two and forward
them. `warm_preparation_timeout_seconds` is **not** computed inside
`_conform_full_asset_into_cache`, because that method is *also* called synchronously from
`_prepare_segment`'s untrimmed-MISS branch on the air path — computing it there would have
silently widened the air-path budget, which the brief forbids. The docstring says so at
`preparer.py:779-786`. **OBSERVED** at `ed5c8464`.

The probe also needs the scaled budget, not just the conform: a probe killed at the flat bound
measures nothing and degrades to single-pass, and per U20 a single-pass artifact must not
occupy the whole-asset cache entry — which is the same permanent re-warm loop by another
route. That is why the two knobs are threaded through `_measure_loudnorm_metadata` too.

### 3.2 (3b) The warm runs at lowered priority — and POSIX did not

Windows was already covered: `run_ffmpeg(..., lower_priority=True)` mapped to
`BELOW_NORMAL_PRIORITY_CLASS`. The **POSIX spelling was missing entirely** — `lower_priority`
was silently ignored off Windows. Fixed in `civiccast/stream/_ffmpeg.py:500-512`:

```python
creationflags = 0
if lower_priority:
    if os.name == "nt":
        creationflags = getattr(subprocess, "BELOW_NORMAL_PRIORITY_CLASS", 0)
    elif shutil.which("nice") is not None:
        cmd = ["nice", "-n", str(_LOWER_PRIORITY_NICE_ADJUSTMENT), *cmd]
```

`_LOWER_PRIORITY_NICE_ADJUSTMENT = 10` (`_ffmpeg.py:447`). `nice` is an **argv prefix** (it
`exec`s ffmpeg itself), deliberately *not* `preexec_fn` — that is unsafe from any thread but
the one that called `subprocess`, and the warm runs on its own worker thread. A POSIX host
with no `nice` on PATH runs ffmpeg at normal priority rather than failing the job: degrading a
priority hint must never fail the work.

Two tests prove the flag reaches the subprocess (`tests/stream/test_ffmpeg.py:514`, `:541`):
one patches the module's `os` to `SimpleNamespace(name="posix")` and asserts the recorded argv
is exactly `["nice", "-n", "10", "/usr/bin/ffmpeg", "-y", "-version"]` with
`creationflags == 0`; the other asserts the no-`nice` case is a no-op. The pre-existing
Windows test (`:503`) still passes.

### 3.3 (3c) A failed warm backs off for six hours, with one warning

`self._warm_backoff_until: dict[str, float]` (`preparer.py:479`), guarded by the same
`self._warming_guard` that owns `self._warming`. In `_schedule_warm`, after the dedupe check
(`preparer.py:1346`):

```python
backoff_until = self._warm_backoff_until.get(key)
if backoff_until is not None:
    if backoff_until > time.monotonic():
        return
    del self._warm_backoff_until[key]
```

On failure the job writes `self._warm_backoff_until[key] = time.monotonic() +
_WARM_FAILURE_BACKOFF_SECONDS` (`preparer.py:1405`, `_WARM_FAILURE_BACKOFF_SECONDS = 6*60*60.0`
at `preparer.py:112`) and emits exactly **one WARNING** (`preparer.py:1415`) naming the asset,
its duration, the budget it was allowed, and the reason:

```
Conform-cache warm failed for %r (%s, allowed %gs to conform, reason: %s);
not retrying it for 6h. Airings of this asset keep serving their bounded
per-segment conform and the full-asset cache entry stays cold in the meantime.
```

The old `_LOG.exception("Conform-cache warm failed; next airing re-warms.")` — ERROR class
plus a full traceback, once per airing, 33 times in the live log — is gone. The reason string
is truncated to the exception type past 300 characters, since ffmpeg's own stderr is already in
the log.

Three deliberate boundaries:
- **`except SourcePreparationCancelledError: pass`** — a shutdown/pause is not a property of
  the asset: no warning, no backoff.
- **Only a populated cache entry clears the window** (`preparer.py:1430-1433`): `None` means
  the call deliberately wrote nothing (per-key lock held by another conform, or the
  measurement degraded to single-pass), which is benign and must not look like a failure.
- The entry is **deleted when expired**, so a transient cause (contention, a full disk) gets
  another attempt on a later airing with no operator intervention.

### 3.4 (3d) On-demand prep never waits behind a warm — proven, no change needed

The air path takes no conform lock and never runs on the warm worker, so there is nothing to
separate. Proven by test, not by assertion: `test_on_demand_conform_shares_no_lock_or_queue_with_a_warm`
(`tests/egress/test_preparer.py:3635`) starts a warm on the real scheduler, drives a
synchronous on-demand preparation on the calling thread while that warm is in flight, and
asserts the on-demand ffmpeg calls completed *while* the warm's worker thread was still inside
its job, and that the on-demand call saw no `_conform_locks` contention. This test **passes
before and after** the change — which is the point: 3d was already true and is now pinned. The
only real coupling is CPU, measured in §2 at +7.6 %.

## 10. Doubts, and what I could not verify

1. **The measurement is a sample, not a survey.** 16.3x / 10.42x are single runs on this box
   under the load it happened to be carrying. The design speed of 8.0 is de-rated *below* the
   worst measurement, which is the mitigation — but a materially slower station would still
   time out on a very long asset, just later. That is a bounded, warned, backed-off failure
   now, not a loop, which is the deliverable.
2. **A degraded measurement still re-queues.** If the warm's **probe** degrades to single-pass
   (U20's failure path), `_conform_full_asset_into_cache` returns `None`, and per §3.3 a `None`
   deliberately does **not** start the backoff — so that asset is re-warmed on the next
   airing. With the probe now carrying the warm's own scaled budget, this requires the probe
   alone to exceed `duration/8×1.5` seconds, i.e. slower than ~5.3x realtime. It is a genuine
   residual gap in the observed-symptom path, the exception path rather than the timeout path,
   and it is **not** covered by the live log evidence (all 33 failures are the timeout shape).
   I chose not to back off on `None` because the other `None` cause — per-key lock contention
   with a conform that is probably about to populate the cache — would then wrongly suppress
   the warm.
3. **The backoff is in-process only.** A station restart clears it. That is intentional (a
   restart is exactly when a transient cause may be gone) and is stated here so nobody reads
   the 6 h window as durability.
4. **Six hours suppresses roughly twelve airings** at the measured ~1800 s re-queue interval
   (**OBSERVED** arithmetic on §6). If the true cause is contention that clears in 20 minutes,
   the warm waits up to 6 h to notice. I judged a long window the right side to err on given
   the symptom was *permanent* re-queuing; it is a tunable constant if the coordinator
   disagrees.
5. **The ceiling is wall-clock, not a duration claim.** 7200 s caps how long one warm may hold
   the single `conform-cache-warm` worker — it is **not** a statement about the longest meeting
   the station has recorded (the longest asset in the live set is 15949.2 s, 4.43 h, and it
   designs to ~2990 s inside the ceiling). Two earlier comments of mine conflated these and
   were corrected before commit.
6. **Both the Windows and the POSIX priority paths are proven at the argv/flags layer, not on
   a POSIX host.** The `nice` tests assert the constructed argv and `creationflags`; no
   `nice`-based conform was actually executed, because this is a Windows box. The Windows
   path's priority class *was* read back from the live child process (§2).
7. **The one `tests/egress` failure is not mine but is not fixed by me either** — pre-existing,
   proven pre-existing by the HEAD-swap in §5, and left alone as out of this unit's scope.

**No `questions\U29.md` was written.** The brief allows one only for a false premise or a
product question; none arose. Every item in the brief turned out to be true of the code as it
stands, the four fixes were all implementable as specified, and the one place the brief's
premise was narrower than reality — 3b, where POSIX was silently ignoring `lower_priority` —
needed a fix inside the brief's own intent rather than a question back to the coordinator. It
is recorded in §3.2.
