and the policy call receives `plan_end_at=None` (`daemon.py:3604-3609`).

**OBSERVED — and the policy therefore returns False.** `reload_policy.py:145-150`:


With `previous_state="FALLBACK_SLATE"`, `manual_override_active=False`, `plan_end_at=None`: the
first clause is false for `FALLBACK_SLATE`, the second is false because `plan_end_at is None`, so the

`control_plane-app.log:22287` (`switch_at_end_of_current=False`).

**OBSERVED — the policy means this, in its own words.** `reload_policy.py:34-41`:

> "Deferring is safe ONLY for an automation-driven extension of a finite ON_AIR or FALLBACK_SLATE
> plan with no operator override in effect. … A FALLBACK_SLATE gap-replan has no tracked
> `plan_end_at` and still interrupts filler immediately; a finite filler-horizon rollover has one and
> defers to that boundary."

and `reload_policy.py:126-129` repeats it at the function level.

**INFERRED — deferring to "the slate's end" would be actively worse here.** The slate plan is 12 ×
30 s = 360 s of filler (`source_plan.py:155-162,205-208`; `MAX_PLAYLIST_SUBCHAINS = 12`,
`models.py:698`), and the horizon rule at `reload_policy.py:145-146` exists precisely to cut
immediately when a tracked horizon is already past. Making a due scheduled program wait up to ~6
minutes for filler to finish is not a defensible default, and the policy already encodes that
judgement.

**OBSERVED — an immediate cut is also the *common* case, not a rarity.** In `stdout.log` the
transactions show 46 `mode=immediate` against 505 `mode=deferred` reload commits, and immediate
slate→program cuts have succeeded repeatedly (e.g. `stdout.log:2591-2592` `old leg disposed` /

`:2671-2672`). So immediate-plus-live-slate is not deterministic-fatal; the incident is a race.

**Conclusion.** The 18:46 program-due reload during the slate was the designed path, and the
immediate cut is the designed switch mode for it. The defect is **not** in that policy decision — it
is in what the immediate path asks the *disposal mechanism* to do (tear down a live 12-subchain
`concat` slate leg inside a 15 s bound). Any fix should therefore leave `should_defer_switch` alone.

Ranked by (defect proven?) × (blast radius) × (does it reduce the next occurrence's cost).

### F1 — Make the operator-facing blocking frame name the real frame (OBSERVED defect, smallest possible change)

**What.** Add the two names that actually appear as blocking frames to `daemon.py:215-228`:
`_dispose_confirmed_old_leg` (the 18:48 blocker, `engine.py:3468`) and

happened here (`daemon.py:2208-2254` produced `engine.py:3151 in _retire_reload_old_leg`, observed at

**Blast radius.** One regex, one tuple, one module. No runtime behavior change; the string is

### F2 — Instrument the disposal step that wedges, so the next reproduction is self-localizing (INFERRED gap, cheap, no behavior change)

**What.** `stage=old-tail-detached` is printed *after* the release loop (`engine.py:3471`), so a hang

(`exit_codes.py:50-54`: the localization tool "for whichever future soak reproduces the wedge") and

**Blast radius.** Print statements on the reload path only. **OBSERVED** precedent for the pattern

**What a check must show.** In a sandbox soak (or the live station during a watched window), a

### F3 — Bound or bypass the synchronous selector teardown on the immediate path (INFERRED root cause, real design work)

**What.** `_dispose_confirmed_old_leg` takes a synchronous `release_request_pad` on a live leg
(`engine.py:3466-3470`) and, in five other dumps, the main loop takes a synchronous
`get_property("active-pad")` on the same selector (`engine.py:2964-2967`). The abort path's fix for
this exact class is to unblock the retiring task first (`engine.py:3614-3621`, called at `:3578`)
because "a retired concat task still held its streaming lock" (`engine.py:3559-3564`); the commit path
deliberately does not flush, to avoid a coupled A/V IDLE barrier (`engine.py:3429-3435`). So the two
halves of this file disagree about the safe teardown of a live leg, and the disagreement is
load-bearing.

A real fix therefore has to do one of: (a) quiesce the retiring leg *off* the selector's lock —
flush/segment-terminate it on the retirement thread and only then release the pad; (b) make the
selector interactions never happen while the old leg is live (e.g. only ever defer, and treat a
genuinely horizon-less gap-replan as a full re-create rather than a seamless reload); or (c) shrink
the exposure by not building a 12-subchain slate leg for a 360 s filler horizon.

**Why it is not ranked first.** It is INFERRED (the dump cannot show the C-level lock), and (a) is a
concurrency change on the reload path — the highest-blast-radius area in this subsystem. It should
follow F2, which is the instrument that would confirm it.

**Owner decision required, and I am not making it.** Option (b) touches the Q3 policy and the
"seamless vs re-create" contract; option (c) touches `MAX_PLAYLIST_SUBCHAINS` and the slate plan
shape; option (a) reverses a documented trade-off (`engine.py:3429-3435`). Per the project's
"closed architectural decisions / surface to the director" rule, this is a director call, not a coder
call.

**What a check must show.** Before/after on a deliberate reproduction: an immediate slate→program
reload on a channel whose outgoing leg is the 12-subchain slate. Acceptance: `stage=old-tail-detached`
and `stage=committed` both appear, the `CTRL output` counter never repeats a total across two
consecutive prints, and no watchdog dump is written. Because a wedge is a race, one green run is not
proof — the check must repeat the reload (the log shows ~46 immediate commits as the population),
**and** the failure must have been observed at least once on the same build before the fix.

### F4 — The cost of the exit, which is the biggest real-world harm (OBSERVED, needs a director decision)

**What.** `exit_code=5` destroys a worker that is otherwise fully functional: it had already confirmed
the selector handoff (`stderr:45310`) and was one disposal call from done. Because it is by design an
ordinary crash (`exit_codes.py:54-59`, `daemon.py:3043-3052`), the daemon **discards** the accepted
reload (`:22309`) and takes the 18:48:11 → 18:52:41 path — 270 s of no signal on a municipal channel,
plus a full re-creation (playlist removed at `:22434`, relay/TS relays re-upped at `:22431-22433`).

Nothing here suggests changing that unilaterally: the "no rate-limited exemption" choice is
documented and deliberate, and the 4.5-minute recovery is the automation's normal restart path, not a
bug in this handler. But the *combination* — a bounded 15 s abort that is treated as a full crash —
is what turns a disposal hang into a 4.5-minute outage. A cheaper exit path (keep the already-prerolled
replacement, or restart only the worker with the new graph) is a product/architecture question for
the owner.

**What a check must show.** Nothing yet — this is a decision input, not a fix. If a decision is made,
the check is a measured dark-window figure for the same scenario, compared against the 270 s baseline
recorded here.
