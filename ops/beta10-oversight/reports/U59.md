# U59 (second, unrelated unit of this id) — government's picture ended 5.18 s before its sound

**Plain English.** At 19:15:49 on 2026-09-27 the station handed over from the government meeting
to the next program. For 38 changeovers that day the picture and the sound ended within 0.57–0.80 s
of each other. For this one they ended 5.181 s apart, the engine's 2.000 s safety bound was
exceeded, the engine deliberately declined to trim (that is the fail-open rule for a genuinely
broken asset), and the aired result was a 4.148 s still frame.

The cause is not in the changeover code. It is in the pieces the *preparation* step had emitted.
Every prepared piece is a `-c copy` slice of a cached conform: `ffmpeg -ss <inpoint> -i <conform>
-t <duration> -c copy`. The audio starts exactly at the requested in-point, but video cannot
resume except at its own keyframe — so every piece is **audio-long by the video's start lag**
(0.85 s measured on this station's own education leg). The engine chains a leg's pieces *per
stream*: one video `concat` and a parallel audio `concat`, each fed by that leg's own pieces. The
per-piece lag therefore **sums**. Government's leg was the day's first two-piece leg: 2.590 s per
piece × 2 = 5.181 s, past the 2.000 s bound, fail-open, and the mux held its last frame while the
audio ran on. The 38 clean predecessors were all single-piece legs, so each paid the lag once.

**The fix is at the layer that creates it:** each piece is now emitted with both streams pinned to
the piece's own video window. The measured spread for the reproduced leg goes **+2.080 s (fail-open)
→ −0.075 s**, 3.6 % of the red, and the video stream is bit-identical across the change.

**The change.** After the existing `-c copy` slice is produced (only on the non-trim emission path),

engine's own docstring (`engine.py:407`) documents the same band from 285 samples on the box.

## Item 1 — the rate instrument, and the two choices inside it

Called from `_maybe_print_output_progress`, i.e. on the same 5 s CTRL output cadence the operator
already sees, and *before* that method moves its snapshot baseline (the ordering is asserted by a
test, because reading the baseline after the move would compare a window against itself). The

a 30 s QOS window, and 3 retained legs.

Two choices are load-bearing and both are stated in the code:

* **`sel` leads the rung preference** (`_U59_RATE_RUNG_PREFERENCE = ("sel", "queue", "in")`). The

## Item 1 — how a 2-segment plan is built, what the automation believes its length is, and why the disagreement is not real

All line numbers below are from the **installed** tree

**How a second piece appears.** The provider is constructed with
`max_segments=1 if gstreamer_engine_selected() else 8` (`automation.py:3087`). `_plan_from` accumulates
`built_seconds` as it appends pieces (`source_plan.py:706`, `:736`) and breaks at

```
if len(built) >= max_segments and built_seconds >= min_plan_seconds: break      (source_plan.py:754)
```

with `min_plan_seconds = lead_seconds` (`automation.py:2350`), the lead coming from `_rollover_lead_seconds`
(`automation.py:1552`) and logged by `automation.py:2382-2396`. Because `max_segments` is 1 and the break
needs **both** conditions, **a 2-segment plan is built exactly when piece 1's remainder is shorter than the
lead (690 s)** — the builder takes one piece, finds it does not reach 690 s, and appends a second.

**Piece 2's declared duration is 1800 s, not 1580 s.** `_segment_duration` (`source_plan.py:1158`) returns
`min(slot, playable)` (`:1191`), the slot here being the 1800 s schedule slot; `_segment_from_item`
(`source_plan.py:1052`) then caps the result, `duration = min(duration, max_duration_seconds)`
(`:1116`), and clamps the outpoint, `outpoint = min(outpoint, (inpoint or 0.0) + duration)` (`:1124`, the
D42 clamp). Senior Citizens June is 9154 s long, so neither cap binds: **declared piece 2 = 1800 s**.

## Item 3 — the guard, read from the engine bytes on the station

**What it measures.** Two judges in `_check_stream_stalls`, evaluated in order:

1. **Flat rule (owns attribution).** A required stream's *airing* running time unchanged for
   `stall_timeout_s` (10 s) ⇒ `output stalled`. `_mux_pad_airing_rt` is the running time of the last buffer the
   **mux consumed off that stream's sink pad** - not a chain-in arrival count, not a queue depth. Its producer
   is exact and is worth naming because the whole item turns on it: `_install_mux_pad_airing_frontier` attaches
   `_make_mux_pad_consumed_recorder(pad_name)` to each mux sink pad, and that handler writes
   `self._mux_pad_airing_rt[pad_name] = buffer.pts` on the mux's **`buffer-consumed`** signal (engine 2496) -
   the PTS of the last buffer the mux actually *consumed*, per pad.
2. **Lag rule, only when the flat half is empty.** `leader_rt = max` airing frontier over the required
   streams; for each other stream `behind_s = (leader_rt − candidate_rt)/1e9`, latched into
   `_stream_lag_since[candidate]` the first poll it exceeds `_STREAM_LAG_BOUND_S = 2.0` (engine line 932), and
   judged only once `now − lag_since[candidate] >= stall_timeout_s`. Message:
   `"{label} stream running {behind_s:.1f}s behind {leader_label} after replacement preroll; forcing switch"`.
   A commit in flight (`suspended`) clears the latch and returns - a switch already under way is never
   double-forced.

**The remedy, and why it must not wait.** `_act_on_stream_stall` does exactly one thing:
`if self._force_ready_deferred_switch(reason=force_reason): return True` - and otherwise prints
`CTRL stall: {headline} ({ctx}) - quitting for daemon restart` to stderr, sets `self._error = ("stall", …)`,
and quits. **There is no wait-longer branch anywhere on this path.** That is deliberate: the force path exists
precisely for a leg that can stop producing *without ever delivering the EOS or the boundary the switch is
waiting for* - the deferred rollover waits on EOS, so "wait for EOS" is not available as a remedy when EOS is
the thing that is missing. `_force_ready_deferred_switch` requires `switch_at_end_of_current and new_leg_ready
and not old_leg_eos and not commit_in_progress`, and `_force_deferred_boundary` further requires
`holds_awaited == 0` and `not boundary_forced` - so it cuts only when the replacement is genuinely ready and
nothing else is holding the boundary.
