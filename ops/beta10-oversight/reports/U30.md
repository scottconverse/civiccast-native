## 1. Work 1 — the freeze escalation

### 1.1 Design

Constants (`daemon.py:343-392`, one block carrying the rationale and the live evidence):

| Constant | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `_FREEZE_ESCALATION_AFTER_HEAL_S` | `30.0` | how long the live window may stay frozen after a *failed* self-heal before the worker is restarted |
| `_FREEZE_ESCALATION_RESTART_BUDGET` | `3` | worker restarts per channel per window |
| `_FREEZE_ESCALATION_BUDGET_WINDOW_S` | `3600.0` | the window |
| `_FREEZE_ESCALATION_EXHAUSTED_LOG_INTERVAL_S` | `600.0` | CRITICAL re-log interval once the budget is spent |

**Signal decision (unchanged from the brief, quoted in the code block):** the HLS live window is the
signal and the only one. The worker's `CTRL output: N buffers` line is deliberately **not** used,
because it advances straight through the freeze. That was an argument from the live logs when the
escalation was written; §4.1 now measures it: the counter's per-interval step falls from ~390 to
~244 (the audio-only rate) and keeps climbing.

**State.** `_FreezeEscalationState` (`daemon.py:563`): `pid`/`escalated_for_pid` are **per episode**
(voided when the worker pid changes, so a fresh worker gets a fresh judgement), `restarted_at` /
`last_exhausted_log_at` are **per channel** and deliberately survive a pid change. Field
`self._freeze_escalation: dict[str, _FreezeEscalationState] = {}` (`daemon.py:961`), polled from the
same place as the U16 A/V guard (poll list `daemon.py:1200-1216`: `_poll_output_av_guard` then
`_poll_freeze_escalation`; the call site is `daemon.py:1215`).

**Gate order** (`_poll_freeze_escalation`, `daemon.py:3055`) — every exit is an explicit
non-judgement:

1. no live worker → drop the bookkeeping (nothing to escalate);
2. channel state not `ON_AIR`/`FALLBACK_SLATE`, or draining → **not judged**;
3. pid changed since the episode began → void the episode;
4. already escalated for this pid → nothing to do;
5. `heal_frozen_seconds` is `None`, or below the bound → not a failed heal.

**`heal_frozen_seconds`** (`hls_relay.py:952`) returns the longest freeze, in seconds, of **any** of
the channel's relays that is in a *failed-heal* state — a live child, `heal_attempted`, whose
progress (`relay.progress_at`, else `started_at`, else `first_seen_at`) has not moved. `None` means
"no live child in a failed-heal state", which is the case where the daemon must not act. Negative
elapsed values are skipped.

**The two lines.** One ERROR per escalation, one CRITICAL once the budget is spent:

> `channel {id}: the HLS live window is still frozen {frozen:.1f}s after the relay self-heal replaced its ffmpeg child, so the self-heal did not restore it (residents are watching a stalled stream). Restarting this channel's worker through the ordinary crashed-encoder relaunch path; restart %d of at most %d in the last hour (%d left in this hour).`

The restart itself goes through the **existing** crashed-encoder relaunch path
(`_process_terminate_bounded(process)`), so nothing new has to be reliable for a restart to be
correct. `4eca5791` added the budget-left clause to the ERROR line (the first cut named only the
count); `65314bc0` is the correction in §2.

## 4. What the live evidence says the stall actually is

Sources (read only): `evidence\government-freeze-0839\` and `evidence\public-freeze-1004\` in the
oversight folder, plus `questions\U30-coordinator-note.md`.

### 4.1 The video stream stops feeding the mux; audio continues (OBSERVED, measured)

The worker's `CTRL output: <N> buffers (+<delta>) since PLAYING` line prints one total per 5 s, and
the `(+N)` is delta-since-arm, so the true rate is the difference of consecutive totals. Differencing
them (`%TEMP%\u30\scan_deltas.py`, OBSERVED this unit, earlier run):

| Capture | progress lines | last interval > 300 | final run | preceding 41 intervals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `government-freeze-0839` | 52155 | index 52145 | n=8, mean **246.1** (min 233, max 282) | mean **390.2** |
| `public-freeze-1004` | 72338 | index 72326 | n=10, mean **243.8** (min 234, max 281) | mean **392.9** |

The step falls by a factor of ~1.60. A healthy mux feeds roughly 30 video + 47 audio buffers per
second (77/interval-scaled); audio alone is 47. 77/47 = 1.638 — the measured 1.60 matches **audio
alone**, so **the video branch stops delivering buffers at the freeze while audio continues**. This
is the arithmetic behind the code comment at `engine.py:2545-2548` ("it kept climbing at the
audio-only rate through both 2026-09-25 freezes").

### 4.2 Why the worker survives it (OBSERVED by reading HEAD, INFERRED as the cause)

`_install_output_counter` (`engine.py:1751-1779`) installs the watchdog's progress probe on the
**mux src pad**, counting `BUFFER | BUFFER_LIST` — an **aggregate over whatever the mux interleaves**
into the TS. `_check_stall` (`engine.py:2587-2589`) treats *any* advance of that single integer as
"output advancing — keep watching" and resets the 10 s `stall_timeout_s` budget.

So an audio-only mux keeps the watchdog permanently satisfied. **INFERRED:** that is why the live
incidents show "worker alive, no exit, nothing in `last_error`" — the engine never concluded it had
stalled, because by its own definition it hadn't. A watchdog that required **every** mux sink pad to
advance would see the video pad flatline and trip its 10 s budget.

### 4.6 Ranked candidate causes (none fixed — see §9)

1. **The stall watchdog is blind to a per-stream freeze.** `_output_buffers` is an aggregate mux-src
   counter (`engine.py:1751-1779`) and `_check_stall` accepts any advance
   (`engine.py:2587-2589`). Measured audio-only rate 244/5 s vs 390 healthy. This matches "worker
   alive, no exit". *Strength: high (code read + measurement).* Fix would be: require every mux sink
   pad to advance; its action ("force the deferred switch, commit, resume output") is what the
   pre-existing WSL test exercises.
2. **A deferred switch that reaches `preroll verified` and never fires has no bound.** Case 4's
   reload 7 (§4.4). There is a `CIVICAST_DEFER_SWITCH_TIMEOUT_S` / forced-switch path, but its
   ordering is the subject of the pre-existing failing test (§6) — touching it would need that test
   resolved first. *Strength: medium-high (log evidence), but the code path is already fragile.*
3. **Post-commit teardown of the outgoing video half.** At `stage=committed` the old leg is disposed
   and the new leg owns the pads; if the new video branch's queue/selector stops being scheduled,
   the mux keeps an audio-only stream exactly as measured. The U30 `chain-in`/`mux-in` rungs will
   name this on the next incident. *Strength: medium (no direct evidence yet).*
4. **A GStreamer-side scheduling/relink fault below the worker's own diagnostics** (e.g. the new
   video queue never receives a segment after the switch). *Strength: low as a distinct cause —
   largely the same surface as 3.*

## 10. Doubts

1. **30 s** (after a failed heal) and **3/hour** are judgement calls, not measurements. The live heal
   cadence observed is 06:27:39 → 06:28:11, i.e. ~32 s per heal cycle, so 30 s is roughly one cycle
   — but a slower heal would spend budget that a slower bound would not.
2. **The restarted channel is down for the restart.** Escalation trades a stalled stream for a
   bounced one. That is the intended trade (the stream is stalled either way), but it is a trade.
3. **A station-wide upstream failure** (e.g. a dead UDP source) would freeze every channel's window
   and spend each channel's budget in turn. The budget bounds this to 3 restarts/channel/hour; it
   does not prevent it.
4. The coordinator note and the captured logs disagree on two points (§4.4). I used the logs; if the
   note's reload ids came from the database, both are true and the note's phrasing is the only
   problem.
5. The `heal_frozen_seconds` contract is "ANY live child in a failed-heal state", which is
   deliberately not "the child the daemon last healed". A channel with two relays in a failed-heal
   state reports the longer freeze — correct for the decision, but it means the ERROR's seconds are
   not attributable to one child.
