## 1. The answer in plain English

At 06:42:48 the government channel was on an in-worker slate while the next title was still
conforming. At 06:43:12 that title was ready and the worker executed a **slate → program
fallback** reload. The switch itself was clean: the picture cut over, the schedule advanced,
the channel stayed up, and there is **no lasting A/V offset afterwards**. What the station
published, though, was missing **3.008 s of audio** — a hole in the middle of the switch,
with the picture running on across it.

The gap is not emitted by the slate, by the program, or by an encoder. **It is emitted by
the mux (`mpegtsmux`) at the commit**, because the commit handed it timestamps that were
~3 s in its own past.

Here is the whole mechanism, and it is one number used twice.

When the fallback arms, the worker publishes the switch point as the **pipeline's running
time measured at that instant**: `switch_running_time = 185.808`. Both new-leg pads are
told to offset their streams by that value. Then the two ends are waited for — the bounded
rebase drain, `_REBASE_DRAIN_DEADLINE_S = 3.0` — and **on this shape the drain is not a
formality**: the mux is still holding the retiring leg's already-consumed audio, and giving
that up airs it. On C12 the drain burned its **entire** deadline (`waited≈3.0s`,
`in video=+0 audio=+0`, `sink_65=2 still queued`): the incoming leg delivered nothing while
the worker waited. The commit then happened at running time ≈188.8 — **3.0 s after the
number it was about to use**.

So the commit released both streams with `applied_offset = 185.808`, i.e. ~3.0 s *behind*
where the mux already was. The mux emitted those buffers anyway. Its output ran backwards by
3 s, and the relay — which is an ffmpeg demuxer downstream and cannot repair a 3 s rewind —
logged **232 `Invalid timestamps`** lines and published what it could.

**Why the new leg's audio starts ~3 s after its video.** The two streams were repaired
differently. Measured on the published fixture: `seg092`'s audio stops at 187.171133,
`seg093` carries **zero** audio packets, and `seg094`'s audio resumes at 190.179133 —
3.008 s of published audio gone, exactly the back-dated run's length. The video loses a
single frame: `seg092` video ends 188.369789, `seg093` starts 188.403122, a 0.033333 s gap
(one frame at 30 fps); frame accounting across the three segments is 60 + 54 + 60 = 174
frames over 5.806667 s = 29.97 fps, i.e. one frame short and nothing else. So the picture
carries the switch through, and the sound has a hole in it. **Why the relay dropped audio
and not video is not claimed** — it complained identically about both (`143` audio, `89`
video), and neither the worker log nor the relay log states a rule.

**Why DTS runs ahead of PTS for ~2.9 s.** The relay's own numbers: the back-dated run's
first packet on both streams is `pts 340722720` = 3785.808 s — literally the arm-time offset
plus the worker mux's own +3600 s base, i.e. the number measured 3 s earlier. Then every one
of the 89 video packets repeats `dts 340989281` (3788.769789) and every one of the 142 audio
packets repeats `dts 340993962` (3788.8218), while their PTS values climb normally across the
run. A transport mux may not emit a DTS that goes backwards, so while it was being handed
PES dated from 185.808 s onward it held the last DTS it had already reached — one constant
value repeated, which is what a clamp looks like, not what a computed DTS looks like. The
pin sits 2.96–3.01 s after the offset the commit applied, and the arm was 3.0 s before the
commit, so the arithmetic closes. **This reading is INFERRED** — no worker line prints the
mux's own position and no relay line explains the pin.

**And it is not C12's fault.** Work item 2 asked. The trigger is the **shape** of the
switch, not the engine revision — see §3.

So the seam carries **three** video times inside 23 µs: the slate's last frame 221.835933 on

### (b) Where the ~11.7 ms grid offset comes from

Measured, and it is a construction rather than a drift (`evidence/video-grids.txt`):

```
outgoing grid period                 0.033333 s
outgoing last frame                  221.835933
outgoing grid, one step on           221.869266
incoming first on-grid frame         221.881000
difference                          = 0.011734 s   <- the brief's ~11.7 ms
```

The live log says why directly (127680–127681): on a fallback there is no shorter leg to trim
to — `ends=[video=none,audio=none]`, `trimmed=none` — so `switch_running_time` is simply the
pipeline's own running time at that instant, **220.381 s**, an arbitrary instant rather than a
frame boundary of either leg. The incoming leg's frame 0 is placed at that instant, so the two
30 fps grids meet out of phase. It is a **phase offset, not a drift**: the two periods are
33.3333 ms and 33.344 ms — 0.7 µs apart per frame — so over a 2 s segment the offset grows by
about 0.04 ms. Corroboration that the placement instant is a rounded number: the first on-grid
incoming frame is 221.881000, 0.589 ms after the switch point, consistent with the worker's

### (d) Why DTS is still pinned, and for how many packets


## 4. Work item 3 — the floor, and why

**The brief's bar (gap ≤ 0.050 s, no clamped video frames, zero relay invalid timestamps) is
not reachable at this point in the graph, and this change does not reach it.** Three separate
reasons, each measured above:

1. **The 3-frame hole is not the switch.** It lies wholly inside the retiring slate's own
   stretch, before the switch point. Neither fence can be the dropper (their decision branches
   were read on the LIVE bytes: both keep in-bounds arrivals), and no fence — armed, unbounded,
   or otherwise — can retract audio the mux has already emitted. Closing it means finding and
   fixing whatever starves that ingress, which is upstream of the commit path entirely.
2. **The clamp, the grid phase, and the dts pin are set by a quantity this module cannot
   see.** All three are properties of each stream's mux `dts` frontier at commit time — audio
   0.078078 s, video 0.054933 s past the switch. `engine.py` contains **zero occurrences of
   `dts`** in 8,436 lines. No edit confined to this file can read that frontier, so no edit
   confined to this file can place the incoming leg's first frames *on* the grid or keep its
   packets off the frontier. Changing that means changing what the worker hands the mux
   (timestamp generation) or changing the mux's policy — a different layer, a different slice,
   and plausibly a different risk class.
3. **The 11.7 ms offset is a construction of the fallback itself.** Because there is no shorter
   leg to trim to, the switch point is an arbitrary instant; two 30 fps grids placed at one
   arbitrary instant are out of phase by construction. Making the phase zero means either
   placing the new leg on its own grid — which *creates* a gap of up to one frame instead of a
   clamp, trading a re-dated picture for a visible hold — or choosing the switch point *on* a
   frame boundary, which moves the switch itself. Both are product decisions about what a
   fallback should look like, not mechanical fixes.

**What this change *does* buy, stated as the prediction it is.** The fence exists because a
retiring tail crossing the pad past the switch point steps the emitted audio backwards
(U56 round 5). The fallback was the one switch that could do that with **no bound at all**;
it now carries the same bound as every other switch, so a late retiring arrival that would
have been re-dated past the frontier is now dropped instead. On the C13 fixture **no arrival
is observed crossing the pad**, so the effect of the changed bytes on that event is a
**prediction**, not a measurement — `evidence/live-healthy-fence-contrast.txt` states this
limit in its own header, and shows the healthy arm (lines 109304–109326,
`target={'sink_66': 1798912000000}`, `cutoff=1798300000000`, `trimmed=audio:0.612`) whose
wait/release pair this arm is missing. What *is* measured is that the arm is unbounded, that
`_mux_tail_arrived` is vacuously true on it, and that the disarm path prints nothing.

**Attribution note, so the brief's framing is not over-read.** The log spans two builds: the
mux-tail fence machinery first appears at line 109304, and all 13 engine traceback frames in
the log cite an older engine. The 3.008 s → 0.085 s improvement tracks the **drain** at the
commit (0.062 s to empty on this C13 event, against a 3.0 s burn with buffers still queued on
the older build), and on C13 the fence armed unbounded and released at once — which is what
this slice fixes. Which of C13's constituent changes moved the drain is **not established
here, and is not claimed**; round 1's own reclamp printed 0 lines in the entire C13 run, so it
did not act on this event either.
