## 1. Headline

The stall is **not** caused by sparse slate keyframes (they are 2.0 s apart, exactly as designed) and
it is **not** reproducible by the hold/rebase mechanics alone. Two separate defects exist, both
introduced by the **leg switch**, and the visible 30 s freeze is the *second-order* consequence of the
first:

- **D1 (currently live, worse):** at a program→slate switch the outgoing/incoming **video** branch's
  timeline is displaced relative to the **audio** branch and never re-converges. Measured live on
  public *right now*: **108.943 s** video-vs-audio PTS offset inside the same segments, playlist PDT
  **≈115 s behind wall clock** (114.6 s at the 20:23:34 sample) — while government (0.0067 s, PDT on
  time) and education (PDT on time)
  are healthy. Source files are aligned (≈0.02–0.05 s), so the displacement is introduced by the
  playout path, not inherited.
- **D2:** when the worker's video branch falls behind the real-time audio branch (timeline frozen or
  delivered late), the relay's ffmpeg HLS muxer cannot cut a segment — cuts need a keyframe **and**
  ≥2 s of *media* time (`-hls_time 2`, `-c:v copy`, so no keyframe can be forced downstream). The
  playlist's newest `.ts` name therefore stops changing for as long as the backlog lasts, which is the
  relay's **only** progress signal (`_last_segment`). At 30 s the daemon's bound fires and restarts the
  relay child — a deliberate **viewer discontinuity** — for a producer-side backlog.

The single reproducible way I found to make the slate leg deliver *almost nothing* for ~30 s (while
the mux keeps ticking at the audio rate, which is what U13 measured: ~47 buffers/s on slate vs ~77 on
program) is a **pad offset that lands ahead of the pipeline's current running time** with a
clock-synced sink: the leg's buffers are withheld until the clock catches up. See §3, A/B control.

## 3. Q2 — How the relay cuts segments, and why the first slate segment can be late

Source: installed `civiccast/egress/sinks.py` (`HlsSink.output_args`), `segment_seconds = 2`,
`playlist_size = 6` (6 × 2 s = 12 s sliding window):

```
"-hls_segment_filename", segment_pattern,

```

Its own docstring (OBSERVED) states the consequence: *"ffmpeg's HLS muxer cuts only on a keyframe, and
`-hls_time` is therefore a target, not a guarantee — the real segment length follows the video's IDR
interval, and stream-copy means this sink cannot force keyframes."*

Full relay argv (installed `hls_relay.py`):

```
_fflags +genpts -analyzeduration 6000000 -probesize 6000000
-i udp://127.0.0.1:<port>?overrun_nonfatal=1&fifo_size=50000000
-c:v copy -c:a aac -f hls -hls_time 2 -hls_list_size 6
-hls_flags delete_segments+append_list+program_date_time+independent_segments
-hls_segment_filename <dir>/seg%09d.ts <dir>/playlist.m3u8
```

Two independent lateness sources, both OBSERVED in code:

- **Cut rule.** A segment appears only when a keyframe arrives **and** ≥2 s of *media* time has elapsed
  in the current segment. `-c:v copy` means the sink cannot force a keyframe at the leg switch, and no
  GOP/`-force_key_frames` change downstream can help. If the incoming video *timeline* is frozen or
  displaced, no cut happens even though frames and keyframes keep flowing (U13's counters).
- **Probe window.** `-analyzeduration 6000000 -probesize 6000000` (6 s) + `+genpts`: at a *relay*
  restart ffmpeg must probe up to 6 s of UDP input before it can decide the stream layout. That
  explains the "has produced no live window" restarts (18:52:48) but is *not* involved in the 20:12
  public episode (no restart occurred there).

Progress signal (installed `hls_relay.py:137` `_last_segment`) — OBSERVED, and this matters:

```
This is the ONLY signal used for progress. Manifest SIZE is deliberately
excluded: ... treating that as progress would falsely reset the freshness clock
on a frozen window (audit finding 1). mtime is excluded for the same reason.
```
Bound: `_DEFAULT_STALL_BOUND_S = 30.0` (`hls_relay.py:106`), with the comment *"The live symptom was
minutes, so 30s is far above a healthy 2s segment cadence and every real muxer hiccup, while still
bounding a visible freeze."* Self-heal (`hls_relay.py:465-531`) restarts the child when the window has
been unchanged for > `stall_bound_s`; the restart is explicitly **not** a seamless splice
(*"restarting DOES break viewer continuity … this is a deliberate last-resort recovery"*).

### Smallest fix (proposal, ranked by size)

1. **Stop paying a viewer discontinuity for a producer-side backlog.** Keep the 30 s bound for a *dead
   child*, but do not self-heal on "last segment unchanged" while the child is alive and the channel is
   producing — instead extend the bound for the states where a disturbed timeline is expected
   (`FALLBACK_SLATE`), and log the condition. This is a few lines in
   `hls_relay.py:maybe_self_heal_stalled` / `_DEFAULT_STALL_BOUND_S`. It removes the *restart*, not the
   freeze — but the restart is currently the only viewer-visible *additional* harm.
2. **Fix the per-branch displacement (C1), which is the actual defect.** The switch must leave every
   stream's delivered timeline continuous: the rebase offset has to be derived from the *new* leg's
   actual first-buffer running time per pad, and be verified per stream, not per leg. This is the only
   change that removes both the 108.9 s on-air desync on public and the freeze mechanism behind C2/C3.
3. **Remove the CPU storm (C5) if it is confirmed** — cap concurrent conform jobs per channel and back
   off the 300 s-timeout retries; the playout encoder must not compete with 300 s ffmpeg children. If
   C5 is the trigger, no HLS-side change fixes the freeze.
4. Explicitly **not** recommended: a shorter slate GOP or `-force_key_frames` — both are inert here
   (`-c:v copy`; and the GOP is already 2 s). A "stall bound that tolerates the gap" (option 4 in the
   brief) is fix 1 and should be understood as *symptom management*, not a cure.
