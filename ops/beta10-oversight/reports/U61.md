# U61 — why every 1800 s slice of an already-seen title took 300–460 s to prepare

Coder: Claude. Coordinator: Claude. Base: LIVE bytes C11 (automation `7b4e4f18`, daemon
`0d723536`, preparer `b19b7a43`). Worktree `civiccast-ds-u61`, branch `beta10-u61`, HEAD
`d6506686`. **No station action was taken at any point in this unit** — nothing installed,
restarted, or signalled; the station's logs and conform cache were read with shared reads
only.

---

## 1. The answer in plain English

The station had already conformed that title. The problem was not that it conformed it
again — it is that **the mechanism that makes a repeat slice cheap was switched off in
exactly the situation it exists for**, so the conform never happened ahead of time and
every slice paid for it in sequence.

Two things, and they compound.

**First, the gate.** `ChannelAutomationService` has an off-air look-ahead warm: at a
dispatch tick it walks the boundaries ahead and asks the daemon to warm the conform cache
for the first one whose own dispatch is still a full lead away. U60 shipped it gated on
`inside_lead_recovery` — a flag that is true only when the plan on air is **shorter** than
the rollover lead. An 1800 s plan under the station's 690 s lead is not that. So the branch
never ran. Confirmed exhaustively for 2026-09-27: **zero** look-ahead lines, **zero**
`warm_source_plan` lines, and **no `warm/` scratch directory under any of the three
channels**, all day.

**Second, therefore, the only warm request that title ever got was the air path's own** —
and `_prepare_segment` asks for it at preparer.py:3093, *after* that slice's cold conform
has already returned. By then the slice that wanted it is airing. So the requests just kept
being made too late, and the single global warm FIFO kept being occupied by whatever else
was warming.

The measurable consequence: **the title's first servable cache entry was born at
23:10:37.863**. The very next government slice, at 23:23:45, hit it in **2.2 s**. Between
the title's first slice (21:24:08) and that birth, government paid **five full cold
conforms** on the same asset:

| prep completed | cost |
|---|---|
| 21:24:08 | 383.6 s |
| 21:49:29 | 406.0 s |
| 22:20:23 | 458.5 s |
| 22:34:58 | 293.6 s |
| 22:58:03 | 267.0 s |

(The brief listed three of these; there were five.) Every one of them carried a
slice-window Speech-leveling line, meaning a fresh bounded re-encode of the window; every
one of the six hits that day carried **none**. That line is a 11/11 reliable miss
discriminator on this title's history.

**Nothing was evicted and nothing was keyed wrongly.** The entry was simply not there yet.

## 3. The deployed rollover lead is 690 s, not 270 s — and the log is misleading about it

The brief's "1800s at 12x + 120s (= 270 s)" reading comes from the **parenthetical in the
log line**. That parenthetical templates the *adaptive* term, which is not the number in
force. Proven three ways:

1. `_rollover_lead_seconds(asset_seconds)` returns `max(computed, asset_seconds / 12 + 120)`
   where `computed = max(120, 2 * preparation_timeout + 30 + 60)`. The station's
   `CIVICAST_EGRESS_PREPARATION_TIMEOUT_SECONDS=300` ⇒ `computed = 690`.
2. The live line reads `22:53:33,965 … lead=690s … (1800s at 12x + 120s)`: the lead is
   **690** while the parenthetical says **270**. They are different numbers on the same
   line.
3. **Every** government rollover line that day reads `lead=690s` — 50 of 50 — while the
   parenthetical takes **14 distinct values** (`1s`, `2s`, `192s`, `290s`, `457s`, `565s`,
   `585s`, `592s`, `699s`, `985s`, `1511s`, `1735s`, `1741s`, `1800s`). A field that is
   constant while its neighbour varies is not the field in force.

A second template exists for the unknown-duration case ("lead=690s (no asset duration
available for the boundary; the timeout-derived lead …)"), which is what the 21:17:42
education and government lines carry.

This matters because it is the whole reason the gate was wrong: `inside_lead_recovery` is
the *short-leg* case (plan shorter than the lead), and under a 690 s lead an 1800 s plan can
never be that. Under the mis-read 270 s lead it would have been — 1800 > 270 either way, but
the margin the brief computed for the fix's benefit was off by a factor of 2.5.

## 4. Why the entry finally landed at 23:10:37, and not earlier

The warm path logs **nothing on success** (`_warm_upcoming_plan` swallows exceptions to
`_LOG.debug`; `daemon.warm_source_plan` logs only on exception; the promotion writes no
line). So the station's own evidence can bound this but cannot attribute it fully. What is
provable:

- `_warming` / `_warm_queue` / `_warm_backoff_until` are **per-process**. The
  **22:30:03–04 daemon restart** voided them, so nothing queued before it survived.
- The **single global warm FIFO** was continuously busy with other channels' whole-asset
  conforms. The two whose lifecycle is fully visible: City Council Aug 11 `757519c3`
  (`.ts` born 21:47:44 → promoted 22:04:47) and NSF Day After Tomorrow `e6b580bf` (`.ts`
  born 22:15:31 → promoted 22:22:18).
- Only a request made **after** the restart could survive to completion, and the first one
  for this asset that did is the air path's request from the 22:58:03 slice — giving
  `.ts` born 23:01:45.660, sidecar born **23:10:37.863**.
- `_WARM_FAILURE_BACKOFF_SECONDS` is **6 h** — longer than the title's own airing — so a
  failed warm can suppress retries for the rest of a meeting.

**What cannot be attributed:** the FIFO's occupancy between 22:34:58 and 23:01:45. Nothing
in any rotated log names it. I am not going to invent an occupant.

## 5. The fix

**One conjunct dropped**, plus a comment block. `automation.py`:

```diff
-        if not force_fallback and inside_lead_recovery and lead_seconds is not None:
+        if not force_fallback and lead_seconds is not None:
             self._warm_upcoming_plan(...)
```

Nothing else moves. The walk inside `_warm_upcoming_plan` still stops at the first boundary
at least `now + lead` out, so it can never race the reload's own synchronous conform, and
the call still sits strictly *after* the dispatch is enqueued, inside the existing
`try/except Exception → _LOG.debug`. `force_fallback`, the `lead_seconds is not None`
conjunct, and the daemon capability probe (`hasattr(daemon, "warm_source_plan")`) are all
untouched. Strictly additive in effect: it can only *ask* for a warm that was not asked for
before.

### The honest magnitude

This buys a **head start of one slice-conform**, not a cure. Measured tonight, a slice
conform is 267–458 s. The air path's request for the same key still fires at the *end* of
that slice's conform; this gate fires at the dispatch tick, which is a full plan-length
earlier. What it does **not** do:

- It does not fix the **single global FIFO**. When three channels all need a whole-asset
  conform, one waits.
- It does not survive a **daemon restart** (`_warming` is per-process).
- It does not shorten the **6 h failure backoff**.
- It does not make a **first** slice of a title fast. Target ≤ 30 s is what a *hit* costs;
  the measured hits were 2.2 / 5.4 / 6.1 / 9.8 / 12.7 / 15.8 s.

### Does the runway actually cover the conform?

Yes, measured — and this is the part the code change rests on, so it is measured rather than
assumed. The promotion is a **rename**, not a copy: `_promote_conform_into_cache` does
`tmp.replace(final)` (preparer.py:1240), and its own docstring calls itself "the SOLE place
a `.ts` is ever renamed into the persistent cache". A within-volume rename preserves NTFS
creation time, so a cache `.ts` **is born when the conform's output was created** and its
sidecar `.json` **is born when the meta is written straight after the rename** — the
birth-to-birth gap *is* the whole-asset conform. (Corroborated independently: the
`leveling (window: full asset)` line, emitted at the start of the ride's full-asset pass,
lands within 0.10 s of the `.ts` birth on `8564fe6c`.) Read that way:

| asset | media s | `.ts` birth → `.json` birth | conform | rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| City Council Aug 11 `757519c3` | 15,949.199933 | 21:47:44.434 → 22:04:47.236 | **1022.8 s** | 15.6× |
| Sustainability `8564fe6c` | 9,020.834 | 23:01:45.660 → 23:10:37.863 | 532.2 s | 17.0× |
| NSF Day After Tomorrow `e6b580bf` | 5,334.814 | 22:15:31.457 → 22:22:18.293 | 406.8 s | 13.1× |

The bench figure recorded at preparer.py:96 is 16.3×. **So the live whole-asset rate is
13.1×–17.0×, i.e. essentially the bench rate, not slower** — the three-channel contention
did not measurably slow the conform. The gate's runway is **1790 s**; the largest whole-asset
conform this box has ever been measured staging is **1022.8 s**. The break-even is an asset
of roughly 8 h, longer than anything that has aired here. `80cdcd4c`'s 110.6 s gap is
**excluded**: its sidecar is probe-only (`full_asset_conform: false`,
`media_duration_seconds: null`) and it is not a whole-asset conform.

### The eviction budget, measured

`_cache_budget_bytes()` = `gb * 1e9`, **decimal**; default 20.0 ⇒ **20,000,000,000 B**. The
override is not set on the station. Resident that evening: three servable entries =
15,647,267,260 B = **78.24 %** of budget (headroom 4.35e9 B). Observed conform density
196,775–684,000 B/s of media depending on content (slide-heavy NSF is the low end,
talking-head council the high end).

**A 4 h conform** (14,400 s): 2.8–9.85 GB depending on content. **One survives comfortably
— 9.85 GB is 49.2 % of the budget at the very densest rate observed.** Two at that rate is
98.5 % of budget, i.e. right on the edge. **Three channels each holding one 4 h meeting
cannot coexist (147 % of budget) and would evict each other oldest-first.** There is no
eviction *line* in the logs to confirm the eviction path was ever exercised tonight, because
it was not.

## 9. What the next lever is (reported, not fixed)

The runway is not the problem; the **scheduling** is. In priority order:

1. **Fairness/priority on the single global warm FIFO.** With three channels, one starved
   worker serialises every whole-asset conform. A per-channel or deadline-aware queue is the
   real fix.
2. **Make the warm request survive a restart**, or at least re-issue it on startup
   (`_warming` / `_warm_queue` / `_warm_backoff_until` are per-process and a mid-title
   restart zeroes them).
3. **`_WARM_FAILURE_BACKOFF_SECONDS = 6 h` is longer than a title's airing.** A backoff
   scoped to a title rather than a key would recover within the same meeting.
4. **A success line for the warm path.** Tonight's entire diagnosis had to be reconstructed
   from filesystem birth times because a successful warm is silent. One INFO line naming the
   key and the outcome would have made this a five-minute question.
5. A secondary code-read finding, not fixed: in-flight scratch ≤ 60 s counts toward the
   eviction budget, scratch 61 s–1 h is counted by neither term, and scratch older than 1 h
   is unlinked (suppressed on Windows while ffmpeg holds the handle) — a 1 h ceiling in
   visible tension with `_WARM_CONFORM_TIMEOUT_CEILING_SECONDS = 7200.0`.
