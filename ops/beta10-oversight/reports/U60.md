## Plain English first

**What went wrong.** The station restarted at 20:46. After it came back, every plan it put on
air was short — 116 s, 290 s, 433 s, 493 s, 667 s — where before the restart its legs ran about
1800 s. A plan on this station is *one* item-slice long (the engine selects
`max_segments=1`, `automation.py:3054`), so a short item means a short leg. The rollover lead
the automation wants before a boundary is 690 s (`_rollover_lead_seconds`,
`automation.py:1552`). For a leg that is only 290 s long, 690 s of lead does not exist: the
trigger `trigger_at = max(last_segment_start_at, plan_end_at - lead)`
(`automation.py:2193-2196`) collapses to `last_segment_start_at`. **The automation therefore
starts preparing the *next* leg at the exact instant the *current* leg goes on air, and gives
the preparation whatever runway is left — the current leg's own length.**

That is fine when a conform is quick. It is not fine when the conform is a cache miss on a
long recording, which on this box costs 208–384 s (the brief's own list; government that
evening took 383.6 s). A 290 s leg cannot absorb a 383 s conform. The plan ran to EOS with the
replacement still preparing, the worker did the only safe thing — it held the slate — and the
government channel sat on a slate card for ~100 s (21:22:29 → 21:24:08) until the reload was
accepted. Then the next boundary inherited the delay, so the lateness latched and repeated.

Two things make this invisible and one makes it self-inflicted:

- The **in-worker slate is not the `FALLBACK_SLATE` state**. The egress state stayed `ON_AIR`
  the whole time, and worse, it kept reporting the *previous* program's name as the current
  source. The public status surface lied by omission for 100 s.
- The automation *has* a recovery path for a slate (`_check_slate_replan`,
  `automation.py:1476-1535`) and it is keyed entirely on `state_row.state == "FALLBACK_SLATE"`.
  Because the in-worker slate never writes that state, **that whole branch cannot fire for this
  incident, by construction.**

**The fix (staged).** When the automation notices it is in exactly this position — a plan on air
shorter than the lead, `inside_lead_recovery`, `automation.py:2207`) — it now looks *ahead*:
it walks the upcoming boundaries, finds the first one that is still a full lead away, resolves
that boundary's plan, and hands it to a new daemon method `warm_source_plan`, which resolves the
channel's stored config and calls `SourcePreparer.warm_plan`. That enqueues a background,
low-priority, duration-scaled whole-asset conform per segment. When the air path later reaches
that boundary and calls the synchronous, air-blocking `prepare`, it finds a genuine cache HIT
instead of paying a first-time conform inline. Nothing on the air path changes; nothing in the
engine changeover changes.

**Why not fix it some other way.** Three alternatives were considered and rejected on the
merits, not on effort:

- *Merge a short tail into the adjacent leg.* The plan shape is one item per plan by design
  (`max_segments=1`); merging would mean changing what a plan *is*, which touches the engine
  selection logic the brief forbids and the schedule's own semantics.
- *Start the next preparation earlier in general.* There is no earlier moment — that is the
  point of the runway law. Before the current plan is accepted, the automation does not know
  which boundary is next; after it is accepted, the current leg is already burning.
- *Raise the lead.* The lead is already derived from the preparation timeout
  (`max(120, 2*timeout + 30 + 60)` = 690 s at the station's 300 s timeout,
  `automation.py:971/994/995/1022/1042/1043`). Raising it would delay every boundary on every
  channel, including the healthy ones, to paper over a slow conform. The look-ahead removes the
  cost instead of moving it.

## What the brief asked, and what happened

### Work 1 — where the short legs come from, and why the automation cannot issue the next rollover earlier

**Answered; mechanism established with cited lines and the station's own log lines.**
Evidence: `staging/U60/evidence/item1-runway-law.txt` (source excerpts plus nine verbatim
`control_plane-app.log` lines recovered by line number).

The chain, in order:

1. **One item per plan.** `max_segments = 1 if gstreamer_engine_selected() else 8`
   (`automation.py:3054`). On the native line every plan is a single item-slice, so **a short
   schedule item is a short leg** — this is not a restart artifact, it is the shape.
2. **The lead is 690 s.** `_rollover_lead_seconds` (`automation.py:1552`) returns
   `max(120, 2*timeout + 30 + 60)` = 690 s at a 300 s preparation timeout, then
   `max(computed, asset_seconds/12 + 120)`. The station logged the un-adjusted floor verbatim:
   `lead=690s (no asset duration available for the boundary; the timeout-derived floor stands)`
   (public 20:47:25; education and government 21:17:42).
3. **The trigger collapses.** `trigger_at = max(last_segment_start_at, plan_end_at - lead)`
   (`automation.py:2193-2196`). When `plan_end_at - last_segment_start_at < lead` — which is
   true for every leg shorter than 690 s — the `max` returns `last_segment_start_at`. The
   dispatch for boundary `b_i` therefore fires at `b_{i-1}`: **the instant the previous plan
   started airing.** The successor's synchronous cold conform gets a runway of
   `min(current leg length, lead)`.
4. **So there is no earlier moment.** This is the same sentence the station itself printed:
   `issued as soon as the previous plan settled ... shorter than the 690s lead a preparation may
   need -- there is no earlier moment`. The automation is not failing to look ahead; at that
   moment the next boundary is not yet known.
5. **The restart produced the short-leg regime.** Post-restart legs were a remainder against
   the schedule's next anchor: public 667 s (20:37:52) → 116 s (20:47:25) → 493 s (20:59:18) →
   433 s (21:15:11); education 289 s and government 290 s at 21:17:42. C9's legs (12:44–20:45)
   were ~1800 s. Each late preparation pushed the next boundary later, so the lateness latched
   — a cascade, not a single event.

`inside_lead_recovery = plan_end_at - last_segment_start_at < timedelta(seconds=lead_seconds)`
(`automation.py:2207`) is the exact predicate that names this regime, and it is only ever set
inside `if self._boundary_source_plan_provider is not None:` (`automation.py:2160`) — so it
implies `lead_seconds is not None`, which is the guard the new walk reuses
(`automation.py:2423`). Its other job is to exempt the dispatch from the D43 cadence floor and
the B2 retry floors (guard at `automation.py:2213`), which is why the late dispatch is *allowed*
to fire rather than being refused.

### Work 2 — the smallest fix that keeps the program on air (STAGED)

**Done, with RED/GREEN in tests and no station involvement.** The change is 392 insertions /
8 deletions across three files, plus 23 new tests.

What it does, precisely:

- **Gate** (`automation.py:2423`): `if not force_fallback and inside_lead_recovery and
  lead_seconds is not None:` — the walk only runs in the exact regime Work 1 describes.
- **Walk** (`_warm_upcoming_plan`, `automation.py:2450`; loop at `2486`): steps forward through
  boundaries using `_resolve_boundary_plan_at` (`automation.py:2509`), capped at
  `_ROLLOVER_LOOKAHEAD_MAX_STEPS = 8` (`automation.py:2448`) — a step cap, deliberately with no
  distance horizon, so a station with many tiny legs still gets a bounded amount of work per
  tick. It stops at the first boundary whose `dispatch_at >= now + lead`, i.e. the first one
  that genuinely still has a full lead.
- **Hand-off** (`automation.py:3122`): `source_plan_warmer=source_preparer_instance.warm_plan`.
- **Daemon** (`daemon.py`): new `SourcePlanWarmerFunc` type (`644`), ctor kwarg
  (`785`), `self._source_plan_warmer` (`798`), `warm_source_plan` (`1699`) which resolves the
  channel's own stored config before handing off (`1716`).
- **Preparer** (`preparer.py`): `warm_preparation_timeout_seconds` (`371`), the `_warming`
  in-flight set (`664`), per-asset `_warm_backoff_until` (`675`),
  `_prepare_segment(..., timeout_seconds=None, lower_priority=False)` (`844-845`), and
  `_WARM_FAILURE_BACKOFF_SECONDS = 6 * 60 * 60.0` (`131`) so a warm that fails does not retry
  in a loop for six hours.

**The look-ahead cannot cost the channel anything.** Every new path is opt-out by absence and
total: no `source_plan_warmer` passed → the walk is skipped and `warm_source_plan` returns
immediately; a warmer that raises, a store whose `get_config` raises, a scheduler that is down,
an unprobeable asset, or an already-warm asset are each a no-op or a log line, and none of them
can raise into the dispatch tick. The warm runs at low priority (`-threads 1`) so it does not
compete with the air path for the box — which matters on a station that is also running an 8 h
rung.

### Work 3 — the slate→program fallback rewind (REPORTED, not fixed)

**Explained from the fixture and the worker log; fix decision is *report only*.**
Evidence: `evidence/item3-relay-log.txt`, `evidence/item3-fixture-rewinds.txt`,
`evidence/item3-fixture-full-scan.txt`.

Confirmed exactly, twice over: the relay logged **5** `Invalid timestamps` input packets and
**5** repairs, from a single relay run (one `Input #0`; ffmpeg n8.1.2 with `--disable-libx264
--enable-libsoxr`). The relay's output timestamps are input timestamps **minus exactly
324,000,000 ticks** — 3600.000 s at 90 kHz — consistent across every packet examined.

The two clusters are different in kind, which is the actual finding:

- **Cluster 1 is a slate artifact.** One audio packet, `size=30`, timestamp displaced 5587
  ticks (62.1 ms) — the largest single displacement in the log.
- **Cluster 2 is the switch itself.** Three audio packets sharing **one frozen DTS
  (`512880037`)** with an ascending PTS ladder (sizes 463 / 381 / 396 — i.e. *program* audio,
  not the 13-byte silent slate packets), plus one video packet (`size=86847`).

The fixture scan is the load-bearing evidence, and it says the rewind is not in the media:
89 segments, 8,259 audio packets, 5,286 video packets, **exactly two backward PTS steps — both
audio, no video rewind, and not one packet anywhere with `pts != dts`.** The two steps are
`seg000001043` idx 49 (−1642 ticks = −18.2 ms, `size=13`) and `seg000001049` idx 33 (−1747 ticks
= −19.4 ms, `size=448`); the neighborhood dumps show each is a single-frame reorder, not a hole.

**Two honest discrepancies, reported rather than smoothed over:**

1. **The brief's "~0.107 s" is not reproducible as a single rewind.** The largest single
   displacement in the log is 5587 ticks (62.1 ms), and the surviving emitted step-back is
   18.2 / 19.4 ms. ~0.1 s is best read as the *aggregate* timestamp span repaired at the switch
   (~92–111 ms depending on how the cluster is bracketed). **The count of 5 is confirmed
   exactly**; the magnitude as stated is not.
2. **Cluster 1's packet (`pts=187802917`) is not present as-written in the fixture.** `seg1041`
   covers that PTS range, but every packet there has `pts == dts`. The fixture is HLS-muxer
   *output* while the relay log reports muxer-*input* timestamps, so a packet-for-packet map
   between the two is not recoverable from these artifacts. That is a property of the evidence,
   not a defect in the analysis.

**Why this is reported and not fixed.** The mechanism lives in the engine changeover path: the
education worker's `reload_id=2` is the only `mode=deferred` fallback carrying
`stage=mux-tail-armed … target=None cutoff=2098605000000` (the fail-OPEN fence, the U56g family)
and `new-leg-first-buffer … applied_offset=2098.605 pts=0.700 running_time=0.000
segment_base=0.000` (the U37/U44 family). Both halves are already-filed, already-adjudicated
families, and the brief says **"No change to the engine changeover code."** Touching it here
would be a change to the surface the brief explicitly excludes, for a 20 ms reorder that the
relay repairs inline. Filed as the follow-up it already is.

### Work 4 — operator visibility of the in-worker slate (REPORTED, not implemented)

**Adjudicated: the finding is real and sharper than the brief supposed; the implementation is a
Critical trigger this slice does not take.** Evidence: `evidence/item4-operator-visibility.txt`.

The finding, stated plainly and with the wrong thing named: **for the whole ~100 s slate window
(21:22:29 → 21:24:08) the daemon wrote `state=ON_AIR, current_source_label="Senior Citizens
Advisory Board - June 2026.mp4"` for the government channel** — the *previous* program's name —
and only flipped to the replacement item at 21:24. So the public status surface was wrong twice
over: wrong state (not on a slate) and wrong label (a program that was not airing).

The reason it cannot be fixed by a small wiring change, in three facts:

- `FALLBACK_SLATE` exists in `EgressStateDb`'s `CHECK` constraint (`civiccast/egress/models.py:310-314`) — the
  state vocabulary is ready for it.
- `_check_slate_replan` (`automation.py:1476-1535`) keys its entire recovery branch on
  `state_row.state == "FALLBACK_SLATE"`, so **that branch cannot fire for an in-worker slate by
  construction.**
- But `EgressStateRow` is `model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")` (`civiccast/egress/models.py:590`) and is
  written through `EGRESS_SCHEMA_VERSION = 1` (`schema_currency.py:18`) with six readers. Adding
  a slate/hold field is a persisted-model change → **Critical trigger**, not a Standard one.

And the honest adjudication of the *incident*: wiring it would **not** have changed this
evening's outcome. `_daemon_has_pending_preparation` (`automation.py:1493`; `def` at `2738`; the U47 slate-first
gate) returns early because the replacement had already been queued at 21:17:44 — the automation
was not blind, it was simply too late, which is Work 1's problem and Work 2's fix.

**Recommended follow-up, cheaper and safer than a model change:** the daemon already polls
`reload-status.json` (`daemon.py:5899-5904`, `civiccast/egress/gst/engine.py:3900-3913`). Let that poll carry a
slate/hold flag and surface it on the channel card. No schema change, no migration, no new
reader.
