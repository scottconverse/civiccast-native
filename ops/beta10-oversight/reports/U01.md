### 3.3 Part A conclusion

**OBSERVED:** the automation thread blocked for ≈445.8 s inside `_start_steps`' pre-yield body,
`daemon.py:1272-1451`, on the path
`_poll_preparation` (868) → `_cancel_preparation` (800) → `_request_reload` (3799) → `_start` (1215)
→ `_drive_preparation` (817) → `next(steps)` (825).

**OBSERVED (and the reason it is not a mystery-lock story):** none of the preparer's conformance
locks can be involved — the body never calls the preparer (structural, §3.2). `_preparation_guard`
cannot be involved cross-thread either (§5).

**INFERRED (most likely single blocker, ranked):**
1. **A filesystem reclaim executed inline on the automation thread** — either
   `SourcePreparer.release`'s `shutil.rmtree` reached through `_cancel_preparation`'s *already-done*
   future callback (`daemon.py:811-815` → `preparer.py:1078-1081`), or the same rmtree via
   `_discard_active_prepared_plan_dir` (`daemon.py:3097`). It is the only unbounded, silent,
   on-path operation that touches a just-written multi-GB artifact, and the disk was demonstrably
   saturated (§4: 1.257 GB conformed in 270 s ≈ 4.6 MB/s; a full-asset conform could not finish
   inside its 300 s cap 12 minutes earlier).
2. **A database call** (`store.get_config` daemon.py:1273, or `store.read_state` daemon.py:3860),
   corroborated only by the `error code 487` child-spawn failure at 13:35:45 inside the window.

**I could not prove which call consumed the 445.8 s.** Two hard facts limit all such stories and I
want them on the record: the block started no earlier than 13:30:44,903 (the other channels were
being polled until then) and ended exactly at a successful `_write_state` (a DB write) at
13:38:10,887. The block is 445.8 s ≈ 300 s + 145.8 s, and 13:35:42.56 = 300 s exactly after
13:30:42.56, when the warm conform that timed out started. I looked for a lock the automation thread
could be waiting on whose holder is the warm worker, and found none — the only shared objects are
module-level `_warm_worker_lock`/`_warm_queue` (preparer.py:175-176) and `_warming_guard` (341),
all reachable only from `prepare()`/`_schedule_warm` (preparer.py:982-1052), i.e. from the
`egress-prepare` executor thread, and the pre-yield body calls none of them. **I therefore record
the 295.5 s overlap with the warm ffmpeg's lifetime as unexplained rather than as a lock.**

**The discriminating experiment for the follow-up unit** (this unit is read-only and I did not run
it): per-step timestamps around `daemon.py:1272-1451`, or a `faulthandler.dump_traceback_later(...)`
/ py-spy stack dump of the `civiccast-channel-automation` thread during a reproduction. One dump at
13:35 would have ended the ambiguity.

## 4. Part B — why public's rollover did not settle in 119 s

**OBSERVED, the 119 s figure and where it comes from.** The log line:
```
3161:2026-09-24 13:28:25,675 INFO civiccast.egress.automation: Channel automation issued a seamless plan rollover for public: the live plan ends in 119s; target=extended schedule before the engine reaches EOS.
```
The lead is chosen in `_check_plan_rollover`:
```
1184:        trigger_at = rollover_trigger_at(
1185:            plan_end_at=plan_end_at,
1186:            last_segment_start_at=last_segment_start_at,
1187:            min_lead_seconds=self._rollover_min_lead_seconds(planned_seconds),
1188:        )
1189:        if self._boundary_source_plan_provider is not None:
1194:            trigger_at = max(
1195:                last_segment_start_at,
1196:                plan_end_at - timedelta(seconds=self._ROLLOVER_MIN_LEAD_SECONDS),
1197:            )
```
with `_ROLLOVER_MIN_LEAD_SECONDS = 120.0` (`automation.py:429`) and
`_rollover_min_lead_seconds()` = `min(120.0, 0.5 * planned_seconds)` (`automation.py:785-814`). Because
the gst engine wires `boundary_source_plan_provider` (`automation.py:1869-1871`, `1950-1952`), the
`max(...)` form at 1194-1197 is what actually runs for these channels: **a fixed 120 s lead**, clamped
up to the last segment's start (`rollover_trigger_at`, `gst/reload_policy.py:153-174`). "ends in 119s"
is just `plan_end_at - now` at the moment of dispatch, one tick after the trigger at
`plan_end_at - 120 s` (`plan_end_at` ≈ 13:30:24, since 13:30:25,482 says "past due by 1s").

**OBSERVED, what happened to the reload.** `13:28:27,978 "channel public: reload source preparation
queued"` (daemon.py:865, kind=`reload`). No completion line ever appeared for it. Compare the working
case 10 minutes later on education:
```
3336:2026-09-24 13:38:33,905 … Content-reload source preparation for education took 8.9s for 1 segment(s) (media preparation completed).
3337:2026-09-24 13:38:34,012 … Seamless content-reload accepted for education (reload_id=16f2fc80-…, switch_at_end_of_current=True); awaiting settlement.
```
So a completion **is** logged when a reload preparation finishes ("took Xs" + "accepted"). Neither
line exists for public anywhere before 13:30:30 ⇒ **the 13:28:27 reload preparation had not completed
inside its 119 s, and never completed at all**: the encoder reached EOS and exited at 13:30:30,160
with `pending_reload=False`, i.e. the seamless switch was never armed on the worker.

**OBSERVED, cold vs cache-hit.** I cannot map the reload's asset to a conform-cache key from the
evidence I have, so I will not claim "cold" for that particular preparation. What is on the record:
* a cache-hit reload on this box took **8.9 s** (education, 13:38:33,905) — comfortably inside 120 s;
* the failed conform that timed out (300 s) was for `…\uploads\lpmrot-04-sustaina\Sustainability_Advisory_Board_-_April_2026.mp4` → `conform-cache\e86530564340230ba4b31798d5a153c3.ts.tmp`, i.e. a **different channel's** full-program conform;
* the conform for public's own restart (queued 13:38:10,892) wrote `egress\public\prepared\4655c1e89954\segment-0001.ts`, **1 257 251 504 bytes, mtime 2026-09-24 13:42:41.147** — **270.3 s** after it was queued (≈4.6 MB/s);
* a further public conform (`prepared\19803d249db6\segment-0001.ts.tmp`) was **still growing at 14:11:46**.

The conform command itself explains the cost (from the traceback):
`ffmpeg -i <program>.mp4 -vf scale=1280:720…,fps=30,format=yuv420p -af loudnorm=I=-16:LRA=11:TP=-1.5 -threads 1 -c:v h264_mf -b:v 6000k -g 60 -c:a aac -b:a 192k … -f mpegts <hash>.ts.tmp`
— a **full-file transcode** to a multi-GB MPEG-TS, with `preparer.py:72
_DEFAULT_PREPARATION_TIMEOUT_SECONDS = 300.0` as its cap.

**Part B conclusion.** 120 s of lead is **structurally too short for a cold conform of these LPM
program assets**: the work is a full-file re-encode whose own shipped cap is 300 s and which in this
run needed 270 s for a successful case and blew the 300 s cap in another. 120 s is fine for a cache
hit (8.9 s measured). So the lead constant is the wrong size for the cold case, and the failure mode
is exactly what happened: the horizon passes, `_check_plan_rollover` can only wait —
```
1088:                    _LOG.info(
1089:                        "Channel automation rollover horizon for %s is past due by %.0fs "
1090:                        "but the seamless reload is still settling; waiting.",
```
(rate-limited per channel by `_ROLLOVER_RETRY_WARN_INTERVAL_SECONDS = 60.0`, `automation.py:449`) —
and the encoder reaches EOS with nothing armed.

An important corollary about that rate limiter: **the single "still settling" line at 13:30:25,482
is not evidence that the check never ran again for the next 60 s**; only one line per channel per
60 s is emitted (daemon.py-side `has_pending_reload_settlement`, automation.py:1033).

## 5. Part C — locks/guards held during the blocking step, and the coupling

Held while the blocking step runs (all on the `civiccast-channel-automation` thread):

| lock | held? | file:line | who else waits on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EgressDaemon._preparation_guard` (RLock) | **YES** — acquired at `_start` `daemon.py:1226`, re-entered at `_drive_preparation` `daemon.py:820`, and the whole generator body runs at `daemon.py:825` | created `daemon.py:459` | `enable_async_preparation` (783), `shutdown_preparation` (791), `_cancel_preparation` (801), `_drive_preparation` (820), `_poll_preparation` (869), `_start` (1226), `stop_all_channels` (4034). **All of these are called from the same single automation thread in the shipped wiring** (`app.py:1405-1409` is the only `ThreadSupervisor` for automation; `platform/worker_runtime.py:63` spawns exactly one thread). No API path calls them with a channel's start. |
| `_preparation_channel_locks[channel_id]` (Lock) | **no** (automation thread) — taken only inside the executor's `prepare()` closure | `daemon.py:845`, `848-855` | only that channel's next `prepare()`; per-channel |
| `HlsRelaySupervisor._guard` | briefly, per call | `hls_relay.py:211`, taken by `is_alive` (320-343) and `apply` (220-247), called at `daemon.py:1285`/`1291` | the same thread's `_poll_hls_relay` (2176) and the API stop path — not held across anything slow |
| caption tap `_session_lock(channel_id)` (RLock) | only for the duration of `channel_start_hook` | `tap_worker.py:777-793`, called from `daemon.py:1337` | **only the caption tap worker, for that same channel** (per-channel dict, tap_worker.py:777-783) |
| DB pooled connections | unknown (pool config not found in the installed tree) | `store.get_config` `daemon.py:1273`, `read_state` `daemon.py:3860` | every DB user in the process: the API, the tap worker's review/retention stores, the 8 `egress-prepare` executor threads |

Other code paths that would wait on the above, and what they actually do:

* other channels' `_drive_preparation` / `_poll_preparation` / `_request_reload` / `_check_plan_rollover`
  / relay polls — **same thread**, so they do not "wait on a lock"; they simply never run. That is the
  coupling.
* `has_pending_reload_settlement` (`daemon.py:3156-3162`) — pure dict membership, no lock.
* the executor threads (`egress-prepare`, max_workers=8, `daemon.py:786-788`) — they do not wait on
  `_preparation_guard` at all.

**Does a per-channel automation thread alone fix the coupling? YES, with one precondition.**
Every lock on the blocking path is either (a) held by the same single thread, (b) per-channel, or
(c) a DB connection. Nothing in the blocked `public` pass holds anything that another channel's pass
needs. The precondition is that the **preparer must stay off the automation thread**: if
`enable_async_preparation()` has not run (`_preparation_executor is None`), `_drive_preparation`
executes the preparer **while holding `_preparation_guard`** —
```
830:            executor = self._preparation_executor
831:            if executor is None:
832:                try:
833:                    try:
834:                        report = preparer(request.plan, request.config)
```
— and a per-channel-thread design would then serialise every channel behind that one daemon-wide
guard for the whole (up-to-300 s) conform. Keep async preparation enabled, or split `_preparation_guard`
per channel, before moving to threads.
