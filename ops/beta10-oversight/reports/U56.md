# U56 — a ~0.6 s timestamp hole at every seamless programme change

Coder: Claude (beta.10 native Windows line). Branch `beta10-u56-av-hole` (worktree
`…\re\civiccast-ds-u56`), based on `beta10-int` @ `521438fe`.
Nothing was pushed. Nothing on the live station was touched.

---

## Summary in plain English

**The picture freezes for about 0.6 s at every seamless programme change, on all
three channels, and audio keeps playing through it.** It is on air now.

The rung caught one of these (education, the `loudness-03` FAIL of
`rung-8h-post-c2-20260926-213112`). I went looking for more and **caught two
more changeovers live — public and government — and both of them have the same
hole, and both of them passed the rung's own continuity check.** The check
catches this defect only when the freeze happens to fall at the *tail* of a
segment. When it falls in the *middle* of a segment — which is what happened on
public and government — the arithmetic the checker does is self-consistent and it
reports PASS while a frozen picture goes out.

So: the defect is not education-specific, not rare, and not a checker artefact.
It is a real, repeated, on-air video freeze, and the instrument that was supposed
to catch it is blind to the majority of instances.

**Size:** the freeze equals the outgoing legs' own start disparity, which the
worker logs as `ends=[video=X,audio=Y]`. Across 289 sampled changeovers that
disparity is a median of **+0.642 s**, with **98.3 % inside +0.5…+0.9 s**. Where
I could measure the emitted bytes, the hole matched the logged disparity to
within **1 ms**.

**Cause (named, in the worker's reload path):** when the reload commits, the fence
that stops the retiring leg feeding the mux drops *everything* it produces from
the instant the fence is armed — but the switch point it is enforcing is the
*audio* end, which is ~0.6 s later than the video end. So the retiring video's
in-flight buffers, the ones that would have carried the picture across that
0.6 s, are thrown away, and the mux gets 0.6 s of audio with no picture.

**Fix:** made the fence *value-bounded* instead of instant-bounded — drop what
the retiring leg produces *past* the switch point, let everything at or before it
through. Implemented, with a RED-first hermetic test (8 of 9 new tests failed
before the change, all 9 pass after). **It is not yet proven to close the hole on
air** — see *What was not measured*.

**One correction to the brief.** The education change was not at 22:40:37. The
capture's `captured_at_utc` is stamped when the window *starts* and the window
fills *forward*, so the boundary sat ~4 minutes later: **22:44:25–37**, and it is
reload **id 7** of education's worker session, whose commit is logged at
**22:44:25.066**. Three independent instruments agree. Details in §2.5.

## 1. What `PTS delta 171000 ticks != expected 108000` actually means

### 1.1 Where the two numbers come from

`scripts/capture_emitted_hls_audio_proof.py`:

```
 678  PCR_MODULUS: Final[int] = 1 << 42
 681  _PTS_FRAME_TOLERANCE_TICKS: Final[int] = 3000
 718      pts_values = [record.get("video_start_pts") for record in records]
 719      pcr_values = [record.get("pcr_first") for record in records]
 720      durations = [record.get("duration") for record in records]
 735      for index in range(1, len(pts_values)):
 736          delta = _modular_delta(pts_values[index - 1], pts_values[index], PTS_MODULUS)
 737          previous_duration = durations[index - 1]
 ...
 742              expected = round(float(previous_duration) * PTS_CLOCK_HZ)
 746              if abs(delta - expected) > _PTS_FRAME_TOLERANCE_TICKS:
 747                  problems.append(
 748                      f"segment {index} PTS delta {delta} ticks != expected {expected}"
 749                  )
```

Both operands are **measured from the emitted bytes**, not read from the playlist —
`_default_probe` (same file, 1255–1330):

```
 result = _default_run([str(ffprobe), "-v", "error", "-select_streams", "v:0",
                        "-show_entries", "stream=start_pts,duration", "-of", "json", str(segment)])
```

So: `expected` = *the previous segment's own measured video-stream duration*, and
`delta` = *how far the next segment's first video frame is from this one's*. The
tolerance is ±3000 ticks = **±33 ms**.

### 1.2 In plain words — what the operator sees

`expected 108000` = 1.200 s of video in the previous segment.
`delta 171000` = the next segment's first video frame is 1.900 s later.

**0.700 s of emitted output carrying audio and no new video — a frozen picture.**
About **21 missing frames** at 29.97/30 fps. The audio does not gap (its PTS
advances straight through), and there is no timestamp *offset* without a gap
either — the video timeline really does run 0.7 s further than the video content
does. It is a picture hole.

Segment 2215 backs this independently: it is declared `EXTINF 1.9` while every
neighbour is 2.0, and it is the only 1.9 s segment in the window.

*(Byte size is consistent with this — 2215 is 255,492 B against a window median of
634,688 B — but size is **confounded** here, because a programme change also
changes the encoder's own bitrate. On the two changeovers caught live the
boundary segment's size sat inside its neighbours' normal range on one channel
and outside it on the other, purely because the new programme's bitrate differed.
**Size is not a discriminator; the PTS census below is.**)*

…and education's `reload-status.json` anchors its per-id commit times exactly:
reload **id 7** commits at **22:44:25.066**, inside every estimate. **The U56
premise is confirmed — the hole is at a committed seamless reload.**

## 3. Cause and fix

### 3.1 Mechanism

`_begin_reload_commit` arms `_arm_old_selector_cutoff` on the retiring leg's
selector pads **before** it reads the observed stream ends, and the probe it
installs is `_drop_everything_probe`. The switch point it then enforces is
`switch_running_time = max(observed)` — and because the audio leg is the later
one, that number is the **audio** end, ~0.6 s beyond the video end.

So the fence, armed at that instant, discards the retiring video's in-flight
buffers — precisely the buffers that would have carried the picture from the
video end up to the audio end. The mux then emits 0.6 s of audio alone.

The installed build's own comment at `engine.py:5030–5032` asserts that "a
sub-frame forward step is absorbed by the mux". **That premise is false: the
median step is 0.642 s — nineteen frames, not a sub-frame.**

### 3.2 Fix

Make the fence **value-bounded rather than instant-bounded**: a new
`_drop_past_switch_point_probe` passes any single buffer whose end running time is
at or before the switch point, and drops everything past it, everything
unmeasurable, and every event/buffer-list. `pending["old_tail_cutoff_ns"]` is
published in `_begin_reload_commit` **before** the offsets are set, so the fence
and the rebase offset are one number from one snapshot. One file,
`civiccast/egress/gst/engine.py`, +80 lines.

### 3.3 RED-first evidence

At the base revision `521438fe`, the new tests: **8 failed, 1 passed**. After the
change: **9 passed**. Whole file: **127 passed / 2 skipped** (129 with the
GStreamer runtime root set). Ordering is asserted as an invariant, not an
accident: the fences are installed *before* the offsets they are bound to.

### 3.4 What the fix does not do

* It can only air the retiring tail **while the selector still reads the old pad**.
  `active-pad` flips in the same commit, microseconds later, and after that the
  old pad's buffers are dropped regardless of this fence. The U37 drain gate fired
  on 35 of 393 commits (~9 %) — so the window in which the fix can help may be
  narrow, and how narrow is not established.
* **The hole was not reproduced on a candidate build, and the fix has not been
  shown to eliminate it in a running station.** No install was performed: the
  brief forbids touching the live station, and the running build is the installed
  one.

## 0. The audit answer first: which instrument produced the false "0 ticks"

The coordinator re-measured my own round-1 copies (ffprobe packet PTS on
`%TEMP%\u56\hot\…`) and found the hole in both — public `seg3655` 0.600 s,
government `seg3711` 0.581 s. So round 1's `questions\U56.md` "CODER RESPONSE"
("0 ticks of video hole … all segments exactly 60 frames") was **wrong**, and it
was wrong for a reason worth writing down, because the instrument that produced
it is exactly the instrument a future session would reach for.

**Two independent defects, both in `%TEMP%\u56\chg_report.py` (132 lines) and in
how it was fed.**

**1. The headline arithmetic measures the join, not the segment.** For each
adjacent pair it computes

```python
vh = cur["v"]["first"] - (prev["v"]["first"] + prev["v"]["span"])
```

— the gap that *spans* a join. The U56 hole does not span a join. It sits
**inside** the boundary segment, between two video packets of the *same*
segment. An internal hole leaves both `cur["v"]["first"]` and the previous
segment's `span` untouched, so that expression returns the healthy one-step value
and the loop prints `0 ticks` while a 0.6 s freeze is in the bytes. It cannot see
this defect by construction.

The script also had an intra-segment census, but thresholded at
`max_step > 3000` ticks — and a healthy one-frame step at 30 fps measures
**3001** ticks, so the threshold sits *inside* the healthy band. Everything
between 3001 and the real hole was reported as "none". That line is the tell to
remember:

```python
ins = [(r["seq"], r["v"]["max_step"], r["v"]["n"]) for r in rows
       if r["v"] and r["v"]["max_step"] > 3000]
```

**2. The copies it read were about 6 s too old.** `catch_chg.py` copies on a
`reload-status.json` mtime bump, immediately, with `COPY_NEWEST=30` taken from
the keeper store — which `bin\segment_keeper.py` writes every
`KEEP_INTERVAL_SECONDS = 4.0` (`:61`) while excluding the newest
`KEEP_EXCLUDE_NEWEST = 2` (`:71`). So the copy ends roughly three segments short
of the switch: public held seq **3623–3652** while the switch segment is
**3655**; government held **3679–3708** while its switch segment is **3711**.
The segment containing the hole was not in the copy at all, so no arithmetic over
that copy could have found it.

**Reuse warning, in one line.** An **intra-segment** video scan with the
discriminator `max_step > 3030` ticks (not 3000 — the healthy step is 3001) finds
this defect; either the between-segment arithmetic **or** a keeper-lagged copy
alone will report a false clean. Both detectors are structurally blind to it, and
"0 ticks" from either is not evidence that a boundary is clean.

### 0a. Reconciliation with the independent session's addendum (below)

A second coder session on U56 appended its own answer to this same question at the
end of this file. Its account is **better evidenced than mine on one point and
corrects my §0 on another**, so the two are merged here rather than left to

**Agreed, independently.** The copy window is the primary cause. That session
names the producer — `catch_chg.py`, firing on `reload-status.json`'s mtime bump
and copying `COPY_NEWEST = 30` from the keeper store at once — and its journal
`%TEMP%\u56\chg.jsonl` **is** the false reading itself:
`{"segs": 30, "boundaries": 29, "worst_video_hole_ticks": 0, "inside_video_steps": []}`.
Its `ls` of the two copies reproduces my §0 numbers exactly (public **3623–3652**
vs switch **3655**; government **3679–3708** vs switch **3711**). Its judgement on
the headline metric is the same as mine: `boundaries: 29` from 30 segments that
all *precede* the boundary "is a control on ordinary playout, not boundary
evidence."

**Correction to §0 above — "structurally blind" is too strong for the second half.**
I attributed the whole defect to `%TEMP%\u56\chg_report.py`. The between-segment
headline is indeed the blind half — in the producer it is
`worst_video_hole_ticks`, the same arithmetic as the `vh =` expression I quoted.
But the producing instrument **does** carry an intra-segment census
(`inside_video_steps`), so the intra-segment test was not absent: it was
**mis-aimed**, and the window defect alone explains its emptiness. The switch
segment was never in the copy, so a correct intra-segment test over that copy
would also have returned `[]`. My reading of the `> 3000` threshold came from a
separate reporting script over the copies; the producing instrument's own threshold
is not what made the list empty.

**The threshold defect is real and shared, but second-order here.** The healthy
one-frame step measures **3001** ticks and the tolerance constant is
`V_FRAME = 3000`, so a `> 3000` flag channel fires on essentially every segment —
the other session measures **219 of 223** poller rows flagged one-frame in its
`poller.py`, and says the defect is visible only above `3030`. That is the same
defect I described in §0, in a different instrument of the same family. It is a
contributor, not the cause: in `chg.jsonl` the list was empty because of the
window.

**The merged rule, on which both accounts agree.** Measure a changeover by the
**switch segment's own sequence number**, never anchored to the trigger instant;
read the **intra-segment `v_max` step**, never a boundary delta; and treat any
`> 3000`-tick threshold as sitting inside the healthy band. Any one of the three
mistakes alone reports a false clean.

# U56 — Report Part IV (round 4)

Round 4 moves the switch point at a seamless change to the end of the **shorter**
outgoing leg. It is staged at `staging\U56c\` from LIVE bytes. Nothing was
installed; no station action was taken.

## Summary in plain English

At a seamless programme change the engine has to pick one instant to hand both
streams over. It used to pick the **later** of the two outgoing ends, which is
almost always the audio end — the outgoing audio runs 0.5-0.9 s past the video.
Everything after that instant belonged to the outgoing programme's video pad,
which had already ended, so no picture arrived and the join was a black hole of
exactly that length. The C4 fence could not help: it cuts *past* the switch
point, and the switch point was the audio end, i.e. already past the video's.

Round 4 picks the **earlier** end instead — the shorter leg — and lets the
existing fence drop the longer leg's tail past it. Measured on the harness clip
that reproduces the live defect deterministically, the hole goes from
**0.572 s to 0.033 s** (bar 0.034 s), 2 runs of 2, and the audio never steps
more than 0.021 s. On a symmetric clip where LIVE already joins cleanly, the fix
changes nothing. Where the two legs differ by more than 2.0 s — an asset that is
genuinely broken, not merely asymmetric — the code deliberately does **not**
trim: it keeps today's behaviour and logs a WARN, so a broken asset stays
visible rather than being quietly cut.

Both streams get the same one offset (the new switch point), so the A/V
relationship across the join is unchanged: video leads audio by 22.7 ms at the
end of the output in the red arm (3615.638656 / 3615.615989) and by the same
22.7 ms in the fix arm (3614.933322 / 3614.910656). No frame was synthesised to
pad the join — every video packet in all 12 runs carries a distinct timestamp.

The measurement item answered a question the audit could not: the asymmetry is
**not** in the source asset and **not** in the prepared plan file. It appears
after the file, at the outgoing leg's own selector sink pads, where the video
arrives 0.567 s short of where the file says it ends while the audio arrives
exactly on the file's end. The remedy does not depend on where the split was
made — it is the switch point's job to absorb it.

## Summary in plain English

fenced the longer leg's tail past it. On the live station that changed nothing:

The reason is where the fence sat. The fence was placed on the retiring leg's own
selector sink pad, and between that pad and the switch there is the
`input-selector`'s one-second, non-leaky isolation queue. A leg that has raced
ahead of the commit has its whole post-switch-point tail **already inside that
queue** by the time the fence goes on, and nothing upstream of a queued buffer
can drop it. So the fence reported faithfully that it had dropped the retiring
tail — the drop decisions were taken — and the tail aired anyway, which is the
live signature: clean audio, and a video hole as long as the tail.

Round 5 puts the **same bound, read from the same published number**, on the
audio pad the tail crosses last before the mux. That pad is downstream of the
queue, so a buffer seen there is a buffer that is genuinely about to air. The
fence stays on past the first selector mutation until the retiring tail has
actually arrived — an arrival reaching the retiring audio's own measured end,
and then a pad that has gone quiet — so the drain's "the pad is empty" cannot
release it early. It is installed on the audio pad only, and that is a

## Summary in plain English

it". On the **mux** pad the same words mean the opposite: the incoming leg restarts
its own timestamps at the head of its own file, so its first frames compute
*below* the retiring segment's start, the probe cannot place them, and it drops
them — as if they were the retiring tail. The fence then only lets go when
something reaches the old audio's end, which the new leg does not do for another
spread. That is the dropout, and the video hole of the same length is the mux
keeping the two streams in step.

### 2.3 The clamp — the switch point re-read where it is final

`_clamp_fallback_switch_point(pending)` runs as the **first statement of `_continue_reload_commit`**,
i.e. after the bounded rebase drain, which is the first moment the mux's aired frontier is final. It
reads `_mux_pad_airing_rt` — written by `_install_mux_pad_airing_frontier`'s `buffer-consumed`
handler, where `set_offset` on a branch pad makes `consumed.pts − arrival.pts == offset` for **every**
buffer, so the recorded value really is the mux's own running time for that stream and is what the
emitted PES PTS carries — and calls `pad.set_offset(frontier)` **only when the frontier is strictly
ahead** of the frozen point. It selects its stream from the fenced stream constant
(`_MUX_TAIL_FENCE_STREAM == "audio"`), and it is inert while stopping and when no audio leg exists.

On the emitted bytes (`evidence/r8-seam.txt`), the shipping candidate's join window shows the retiring
audio running to its last packet, video unbroken across the same instant, and the incoming head at
**exactly** that value:

